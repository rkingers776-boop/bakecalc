Old-fashioned rolled, quick, instant, and steel-cut — they all fill a cup differently. Here's the gram weight for each.

*1 cup old-fashioned rolled oats = 100g when lightly spooned. Steel-cut oats are much heavier: 160g/cup.

1 Cup Rolled Oats = 100 Grams. The Other Types? Not Even Close.

Old-fashioned rolled oats — the kind in the Quaker cylinder with the smiling man on it — weigh 100 grams per US cup. They're steamed, rolled flat, and dried. The flakes are about the size of a fingernail clipping, and they don't pack particularly tightly in a measuring cup. Which is why they only weigh 100g — less than all-purpose flour (120g), less than sugar (200g), less than almost any other dry baking ingredient.

But "oats" isn't one ingredient. Quick oats are cut smaller before rolling and pack slightly tighter at 95g per cup. Steel-cut oats are whole groats chopped with a blade — no rolling, no steaming — and they're dense little nuggets at 160g per cup. If a recipe calls for "1 cup oats" and you grab steel-cut by mistake, you've just added 60% more oat material than intended. Your cookies will be rock-hard pebble-studded disasters. Ask me how I know.

Oatmeal Cookies: Rolled vs Quick — A Side-by-Side

I baked the same cookie recipe twice, once with rolled oats (100g/cup) and once with quick oats (95g/cup) — both measured by weight so the comparison was fair. The rolled oat cookies spread less, had visible oat flakes, and had that distinct chewy-ribbon texture you expect from a classic oatmeal cookie. The quick oat cookies spread more (less structure from the smaller oat pieces), were softer throughout with no distinct flakes, and tasted slightly sweeter because the starch in the smaller oat pieces gelatinized more completely during baking. Both were good. The rolled oat version is what most people picture when they think "oatmeal cookie." The quick oat version is what you get from those frosted oatmeal cookies at the grocery store — soft, uniform, almost cakey. Pick your fighter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 cup of rolled oats?

1 US cup of old-fashioned rolled oats = 100 grams (lightly spooned, not packed). Quick oats: 95g per cup. Steel-cut oats: 160g per cup.

Q2: Can I substitute quick oats for rolled oats in cookies?

By weight (100g either way): yes. By volume: 1 cup of quick oats (95g) is 5% less oat than 1 cup of rolled (100g) — close enough for most recipes. The texture will be softer and less chewy with quick oats. For the most reliable results across oat types, weigh them.

Q3: Are "old-fashioned oats" and "rolled oats" the same thing?

Yes — "old-fashioned oats" is Quaker's brand name for standard rolled oats. They're the same product. Both are whole oat groats that have been steamed and rolled flat, weighing 100g per US cup.
