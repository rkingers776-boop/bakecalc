1/4 Cup Butter to Grams Converter

Convert ¼ US cup of butter into grams — half a stick, 4 tablespoons, or 57 grams at a glance.

How Many Grams in 1/4 Cup of Butter?

Here's a fun fact: of all the quarter-cup conversions in your kitchen, butter is the only one where the package does the measuring for you. ¼ cup of standard stick butter = 56.75 grams (baker-rounds to 57 g), which is exactly 4 tablespoons — half of one American stick. The paper wrapper is printed with tablespoon lines, so you don't touch a measuring cup. Just cut where the wrapper says 4 tbsp and you're done. But the gram number still earns its keep: when a European recipe calls for 57 g of butter and your brain goes blank, or when you're halving a recipe that was originally written for a full cup (227 g) or a half cup (113 g), the scale doesn't lie and the wrapper lines only go so far.

The Conversion Math (Why It's Not Just 227 ÷ 4)

A standard stick of butter (½ cup) weighs 113.4 grams. Half of that — a quarter cup — gives you 56.7 grams. Bakers round to 57 g because nobody's kitchen scale reads to a tenth of a gram and butter has enough natural water-content variation that the decimal doesn't matter. But different butter states throw the number around:

  • Grams = Cups × Grams-per-Cup
  • Stick Butter: 0.25 × 227 g = 56.75 g (round to 57 g, 4 tbsp, ½ stick)
  • Melted Butter: 0.25 × 240 g = 60 grams
  • Whipped Butter: 0.25 × 150 g = 38 grams

Small Volumes, Big Consequences

A 2-gram error at the full-cup level is a rounding footnote — under 1% of the total fat. At the quarter-cup level, those same 2 grams are nearly 4% of your butter, and in a recipe that only calls for a quarter cup total (like a small-batch cookie recipe or a butter wash for pie crust), that percentage is your margin for error. If you accidentally use melted butter (60 g) when the recipe means solid (57 g), you've swapped in 5% more fat by weight and changed the water content — melted butter has lost evaporation moisture that solid butter still holds. Your cookies will spread faster and brown differently. At larger volumes like a full cup, the proportional error shrinks; at ¼ cup, it's amplified. This is why pastry chefs weigh everything and home bakers sometimes wonder why their half-batch brownies didn't turn out like the full batch.

The Whipped Butter Trap

Whipped butter is stick butter with air beaten into it — that's it. No different ingredients, no special process. Just air. And air weighs nothing, which is why a cup of whipped butter clocks in at about 150 g vs stick butter's 227 g. If a recipe says "¼ cup butter" and you're holding a tub of whipped, you've got a problem: ¼ cup of whipped butter is just 38 g, containing roughly the fat of 2.5 tablespoons of stick butter. You'd need a heaping ⅓ cup of whipped butter to hit the same fat content. My advice: save whipped butter for toast. For baking, stick butter measured by weight removes every variable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1/4 cup of butter exactly?

A1: 56.75 grams, which baker-rounds to 57g. That's half a US stick of butter — exactly 4 tablespoons. The butter wrapper already has tablespoon markings, so you can measure 1/4 cup without a measuring cup at all.

Q2: Can I just cut a stick of butter in half for 1/4 cup?

A2: Yes — each US stick is 1/2 cup (8 tbsp), so cutting it crosswise at the 4-tablespoon mark gives you exactly 1/4 cup or 57g. No math, no scale, no measuring cup. The wrapper lines do the work.

Q3: What if I only have whipped butter — how many grams for 1/4 cup?

A3: Whipped butter is about 33% lighter — 1/4 cup weighs roughly 38g. You'd need closer to 1/3 cup of whipped butter to match the fat content of 1/4 cup stick butter. For baking, stick with stick butter and weigh it on a scale. Whipped butter belongs on pancakes, not in your cookie dough.
