3 oz to mL Converter

Convert 3 US fluid ounces to millilitres — and untangle the nutrition label 3 oz, which has nothing to do with fluid ounces.

Milliliter Equivalent:

88.72 mL

*Based on standard US Customary fluid ounce calculations (1 fl oz ≈ 29.57353 mL).

3 oz in mL — the short answer

Three US fluid ounces is 88.72 mL — 3 × 29.5735 = 88.7206. Under the imperial fluid ounce the same figure is 85.24 mL. As always with this conversion, the number is only half the answer; the other half is knowing which ounce the recipe means.

The First Ounce Figure That Misses Every Cup

One, two, four and eight ounces all divide neatly into a US cup, because they are factors of eight. Three ounces sits between a quarter cup and a half cup and matches neither. That single fact explains why 3 fl oz is rare in recipes: there is no measuring cup that reads it directly.

The workaround is tablespoons. Three fluid ounces is 6 tablespoons, or three eighths of a cup. Six spoons is not hard to count, but nobody designs a recipe around a quantity that needs counting.

So 3 oz shows up far more often in drinks, medicine and baby bottles than in baking — places where the container is already sized, and the figure describes a portion rather than something you measure out.

Where 3 oz Actually Appears

  • Baby bottles — 2 oz and 4 oz are the printed marks; 3 oz is the common real-world feed that falls between them.
  • Small-format desserts — a pair of ramekins, or a single large one.
  • Cold brew concentrate — small bottles are often labelled 3 oz of concentrate to be diluted.
  • Cocktails — a small number of large-format recipes use 3 oz as the total spirit ceiling.

Why 3 oz of Meat Is Not 3 Fluid Ounces

On a nutrition label, 3 oz of meat is wrong twice over as a volume. The ounce there is a weight — 85 grams — and it refers to cooked weight. Meat loses water as it cooks, so 3 oz of cooked meat starts out as roughly 4 to 4.5 oz raw. Neither figure has anything to do with fluid ounces.

The same three-letter abbreviation is doing three different jobs across a kitchen:

  • A recipe calling for 3 fl oz means volume — 88.72 mL.
  • A nutrition label reading 3 oz of meat means weight — 85 g, cooked.
  • A pharmacy bottle labelled 3 oz describes container capacity — about 90 mL — while the dose itself will be given in millilitres or teaspoons, never ounces.

The test is whether the label carries the letters fl. With them, you have a volume. Without them, you have a weight.

Measuring 3 oz With What You Already Own

If the only tools in the drawer are cup measures, build it in two steps: a level quarter cup is 4 tablespoons, and two more tablespoons brings you to 6, which is 3 fl oz. That is exact, not approximate.

The comparison that matters is against the neighbour. If a recipe wants half a cup — 4 fl oz, or 118.29 mL — and you supply 3 oz instead, you are short 29.57 mL, almost exactly 25%. In a dough that changes hydration enough to change the bread. This is not a quantity to estimate by eye.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many mL is 3 oz?

A1: 88.72 mL for 3 US fluid ounces. Under the imperial fluid ounce used in Britain and Australia, 3 oz is 85.24 mL, about 4% less.

Q2: How many tablespoons is 3 oz?

A2: Six. One fluid ounce equals 2 tablespoons, so 3 fluid ounces is 6 tablespoons, which is also three eighths of a US cup.

Q3: Does 3 oz of meat mean 3 fluid ounces?

A3: No. Meat is measured by weight, so 3 oz there means 85 grams — and it refers to cooked weight. Because cooking drives off water, 3 oz of cooked meat starts as roughly 4 to 4.5 oz raw. No fluid ounce is involved anywhere in that label.
