50 g of powdered sugar in cups — the short answer

Fifty grams of powdered sugar is 0.42 cups, which is close enough to 2/5 of a cup to measure as 6 tablespoons plus 2 teaspoons. That last form is the one to use, because no measuring cup set contains a 2/5 cup and a spoon count is exact.

The number looks odd for a reason worth knowing, and it is not the fault of the sugar. It is the fault of the cup.

Why 50 g Is Not 1/4 Cup

Fifty grams of granulated sugar is a quarter cup. Fifty grams of powdered sugar is two fifths of a cup. Same weight, 67% more volume.

The two sugars are chemically almost identical — powdered sugar is granulated sugar milled finer and mixed with a little cornstarch. What changes is the packing. A cup of granulated sugar weighs 200 g because the crystals nest together and leave only small gaps. A cup of powdered sugar weighs 120 g because the particles are ground to roughly 10 to 15 microns, and a powder that fine traps a great deal of air between its particles. Air weighs nothing but it takes up room, and the room is what the measuring cup is actually measuring.

This is why a recipe that swaps one sugar for the other by cup produces something different. Two fifths of a cup of powdered sugar is 50 g. Two fifths of a cup of granulated sugar is 80 g. The second version is 60% sweeter, and it will also carry less cornstarch, which matters in a glaze.

Sift It and the Answer Changes

Powdered sugar is sold already lumpy, because it settles in the bag and the cornstarch holds the lumps together. Sifting breaks them up and adds even more air. The density falls from about 120 g per cup to about 100 g per cup.

  • 50 g, unsifted — 0.42 cups, or 6 tbsp + 2 tsp
  • 50 g, sifted — 0.50 cups, or 8 tbsp, exactly half a cup

The weight did not move. The volume did. If a frosting recipe was written by someone holding a sifter, their half cup is your six and two thirds tablespoons, and if you measure the way they did the numbers agree.

How to Measure 50 g Without a Scale

Spoon the sugar into the cup rather than digging the cup into the bag, then level it with the flat edge of a knife. Two habits matter more than the technique itself:

  • Do not tap the cup on the counter. Settling the powder can pack a cup in by 15%, which turns 120 g into 140 g and makes your 50 g portion short by a fifth.
  • Do not press. Powdered sugar holds the shape of whatever presses it, and a pressed cup is not the cup the recipe assumes.

Then take the six tablespoons and the two teaspoons. It sounds fussy and takes about fifteen seconds.

What 50 g of Powdered Sugar Is Actually For

  • Dusting — 50 g is generous for finishing a 9 inch cake; a fine sieve needs only about a third of it.
  • A thin glaze — 50 g beaten with 2 teaspoons of milk or lemon juice gives a pourable glaze for a loaf cake.
  • Royal icing, small batch — enough to flood a dozen cookies, not enough for a tiered project.
  • Sweetening whipped cream — though here a tablespoon at a time is easier, and the cornstarch in powdered sugar does stabilise the foam.

It Is Not Simply Ground-Up Sugar

Powdered sugar carries roughly 3 to 5% cornstarch, added as an anti-caking agent. That cornstarch is why the sugar thickens a liquid it is stirred into, and it is why blitzing granulated sugar in a blender is a poor substitute in a glaze: you get the fineness without the starch, and the glaze stays thin and slightly gritty.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 50g of powdered sugar?

A1: 0.42 cups, which is about 2/5 of a cup, or 6 tablespoons plus 2 teaspoons. The figure assumes unsifted powdered sugar at 120 grams per cup.

Q2: Why is 50g of powdered sugar more than 1/4 cup?

A2: Because powdered sugar is far less dense than granulated. A cup of it weighs 120 grams against 200 for granulated, so the same 50 grams needs 67% more space to sit in.

Q3: Should I sift before measuring 50g?

A3: If you are weighing on a scale, sifting makes no difference to the mass. If you are measuring by cup, sift first and you will need exactly half a cup, because sifted powdered sugar drops to about 100 grams per cup.
