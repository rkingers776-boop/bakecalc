Understanding 100 Grams of Sugar in Recipe Scaling

At 100 grams, you are dealing with exactly 1/2 cup (0.50 cups precisely). Because sugar types vary dramatically in density — granulated is dense and crystalline, powdered is light and aerated, and brown sugar contains sticky molasses — knowing the exact conversion for your specific sweetener is essential for consistent results.

The Mathematical Conversion Formula

The calculation divides the sugar mass by the specific density constant for each variety:

  • Total Cup Volume = Given Weight in Grams ÷ Base Ingredient Unit per Cup
  • Granulated White: 100g ÷ 200g/cup = 0.50 cups (1/2 cup)
  • Packed Brown: 100g ÷ 220g/cup = 0.45 cups
  • Powdered: 100g ÷ 120g/cup = 0.83 cups

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 100g of granulated white sugar?

A1: 100 grams of granulated white sugar equals exactly half a US customary cup (0.50 cups).

Q2: How do I measure 100g of sugar with standard kitchen cups?

A2: Use a single level 1/2 cup measure of granulated white sugar to achieve roughly 100 grams.

Q3: What if my recipe uses a different sugar type for 100g?

A3: Granulated sugar is forgiving because it does not compress easily. However, using packed brown sugar instead will shift the weight ratio by about 10-15%.
