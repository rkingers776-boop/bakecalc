Why Generate a Nutrition Label for Your Bakes?

If you sell baked goods at farmers markets, bake sales, or online — or if you're meal-prepping and want to know what's actually in your banana bread — a nutrition label answers the question everyone asks: "how many calories is that?" This generator takes your recipe ingredients and produces a standard FDA-format label in seconds. No account, no app, no uploads. Everything runs in your browser.

How to Use It

  1. Add each ingredient — type the name (auto-suggested from 45+ baking ingredients) and enter the amount.
  2. Set your servings — how many slices or portions the recipe makes.
  3. Generate & print — the FDA-style label appears instantly; hit Print Label for a clean black-and-white page.

Units are flexible: grams, kilograms, ounces, pounds, milliliters, cups, tablespoons, and teaspoons. For baking powder, salt, and other teaspoon-measured leaveners, the generator uses ingredient-specific teaspoon weights so small amounts stay accurate.

What the % Daily Value Means

The %DV is based on a 2,000-calorie daily diet and tells you how much a single serving contributes to your daily needs. For example, if the label shows Total Fat 20%, that serving provides one-fifth of your recommended daily fat intake. The FDA updates these reference values periodically — this tool uses the current FDA daily values (78g fat, 275g carbs, 50g protein, 2,300mg sodium, and so on).

Data Sources & Accuracy

Ingredient values come from USDA FoodData Central, the same database powering MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, and most nutrition platforms. Because ingredients naturally vary (a large egg vs a jumbo egg, packed vs spooned flour), treat results as solid estimates — within about ±10-15% of a lab analysis. For products you sell commercially in the US, FDA regulations require testing in an accredited laboratory; this tool is designed for recipe planning, meal prep, and home baking.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this label legally compliant for selling baked goods?

No. This tool produces an educational estimate in FDA format for planning purposes. If you sell products commercially in the US, the FDA requires nutrition information from an accredited laboratory analysis, and full compliance involves additional regulations (food allergen labeling, facility registration, etc.). Use this generator to understand and plan your recipe's nutrition — then work with a lab for your actual labels.

Can I add ingredients that aren't in the list?

You can type any ingredient name, but nutrition values only calculate for the 45+ ingredients with USDA data built in. We're expanding the database regularly — if there's a baking ingredient you use often, tell us and we'll add it.

Does cooking change the nutrition values?

Water loss during baking concentrates nutrients per gram but doesn't destroy calories — the label reflects the raw ingredient weights you entered, which is the standard way recipes are analyzed. For most home baking (cakes, cookies, breads), the difference is small and your label stays a solid estimate.
