1 Teaspoon of Instant Yeast to Grams — The Yeast Conversion Table You'll Actually Use

Instant, active dry, and fresh yeast — exact gram weights per teaspoon, plus the substitution math that works every time.

Gram Equivalent:

3.1 grams (3.1 g)

*1 tsp instant or active dry yeast = 3.1g. One 7g packet = 2¼ tsp. Fresh yeast is roughly 3× heavier per tsp.

1 Teaspoon Yeast = 3.1 Grams. The Number Behind Every Loaf of Bread You've Ever Made.

Instant yeast and active dry yeast both weigh 3.1 grams per level US teaspoon. A standard grocery-store packet — the little foil envelope that comes three to a strip — contains 7 grams, which is exactly 2¼ teaspoons. Most home bread recipes call for one packet (7g, 2¼ tsp) per 3-4 cups of flour. That's the baseline. From there, you adjust: less yeast + longer rise = more flavor. More yeast + shorter rise = faster bread with less character. The gram amount stays the same whether you're using instant or active dry — they're interchangeable by weight.

Swapping Between Yeast Types — The Only Formula You Need

Instant → Active Dry: 1:1 by weight. 3.1g of instant = 3.1g of active dry = 1 tsp of either. The two are functionally identical once they're in the dough.

Fresh → Instant: divide by 3. A recipe calling for 30g of fresh yeast needs 10g of instant. A standard 0.6 oz (17g) fresh yeast cake equals roughly 5.7g of instant — which is about 1¾ teaspoons.

Instant → Fresh: multiply by 3. 7g instant yeast = 21g fresh yeast. This is why fresh yeast recipes have such large gram numbers — bakers aren't using more leavening power, they're just using a less concentrated product.

SAF Gold — The Exception to Everything Above

If you bake enriched doughs — brioche, panettone, cinnamon rolls with lots of butter and sugar — there's a specific yeast for that: SAF Gold (osmophilic instant yeast). It's formulated to perform in high-sugar, high-fat environments that would slow down regular instant yeast. The weight is the same (3.1g per tsp), but you use more of it — about 50% more than the recipe's yeast amount — because enriched doughs are harder for yeast to work in. For a brioche that calls for 7g of regular instant yeast, use about 10g of SAF Gold. It's not a brand loyalty thing; it's actual chemistry. Regular yeast in a 30%-butter dough struggles; osmophilic yeast was bred for it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 teaspoon of instant yeast?

1 US teaspoon of instant yeast = 3.1 grams. Active dry yeast weighs the same — 3.1g per teaspoon. A standard 7g grocery store packet contains 2¼ teaspoons.

Q2: Can I use instant yeast instead of active dry yeast?

By weight: yes — 3.1g of either is interchangeable. By volume: instant yeast has slightly more leavening power per teaspoon because of finer granules, so use about 25% less instant yeast when substituting by teaspoons (¾ tsp instant yeast ≈ 1 tsp active dry). But honestly, if you have a scale, just weigh it and avoid the conversion entirely.

Q3: How do I know if my yeast is still alive?

Stir 1 teaspoon (3.1g) of yeast and a pinch of sugar into ¼ cup of warm (100-110°F / 38-43°C) water. Within 10 minutes, it should foam and smell yeasty — not like beer, but like bread dough. No foam = dead yeast. Don't use it. Dead yeast won't hurt you but it won't raise bread, and there's nothing sadder than a dense, flat loaf that you spent hours making. Test it before you commit the flour.
