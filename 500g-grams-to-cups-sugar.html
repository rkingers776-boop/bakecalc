Understanding 500 Grams of Sugar in Recipe Scaling

At 500 grams, you are dealing with 2 cups plus 1/2 cup (exactly 2.50 cups). Because sugar types vary dramatically in density — granulated is dense and crystalline, powdered is light and aerated, and brown sugar contains sticky molasses — knowing the exact conversion for your specific sweetener is essential for consistent results.

The Mathematical Conversion Formula

The calculation divides the sugar mass by the specific density constant for each variety:

  • Total Cup Volume = Given Weight in Grams ÷ Base Ingredient Unit per Cup
  • Granulated White: 500g ÷ 200g/cup = 2.50 cups (2 1/2 cups)
  • Packed Brown: 500g ÷ 220g/cup = 2.27 cups
  • Powdered: 500g ÷ 120g/cup = 4.17 cups

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 500g of granulated white sugar?

A1: 500 grams of granulated white sugar converts to exactly 2.50 cups — two full cups plus one 1/2 cup.

Q2: How do I measure 500g of sugar with standard kitchen cups?

A2: Combine two level 1-cup measures with one level 1/2-cup measure of granulated sugar.

Q3: What if my recipe uses a different sugar type for 500g?

A3: 500g is a bulk baking milestone. At this weight, a digital scale is strongly recommended because cumulative scooping errors across 2.5 cups can drift by 15-25 grams.
