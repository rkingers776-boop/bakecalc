2 oz to mL Converter

Convert 2 US fluid ounces to millilitres — and see why this is one of the few fluid ounce figures that lines up neatly with cups.

Milliliter Equivalent:

59.15 mL

*Based on standard US Customary fluid ounce calculations (1 fl oz ≈ 29.57353 mL).

2 oz in mL — the short answer

Two US fluid ounces is 59.15 mL — 2 × 29.5735 = 59.1471. British and Australian recipes use the smaller imperial fluid ounce, where 2 oz comes to 56.83 mL, about 4% less. If the source is unclear, that difference is unlikely to be the thing that ruins dinner. It is still worth knowing which of the two you are holding.

Two Fluid Ounces Is Exactly One Quarter Cup

This is one of the rare places where US fluid ounces and US cups line up perfectly. A US cup holds 8 fluid ounces, so a quarter cup is 2 fluid ounces, and both equal 59.1471 mL. No rounding, no approximation — the same quantity described two ways.

Every factor of eight behaves this way: 1 oz is 1/8 cup, 2 oz is 1/4 cup, 4 oz is 1/2 cup, 8 oz is a full cup. Step off that grid and the neatness disappears. Three ounces, five ounces and six ounces match no cup mark at all, which is a large part of why they show up so rarely in recipes.

The practical payoff: if a recipe calls for 2 fl oz and you have nothing marked in fluid ounces, reach for the 1/4 cup measure or count out 4 tablespoons. You will be exact rather than close.

Where 2 oz Shows Up

  • Espresso — a standard single shot is about 1 fl oz, so a doppio, the double, is 2 fl oz. This is the one place in a kitchen where 2 oz is a named quantity rather than a conversion.
  • Baby bottles — 2 oz is a common early feeding volume and is printed on the side of nearly every bottle made.
  • Cocktails — 2 oz of spirit is the backbone of a large share of classic recipes, and the place where an accidental 4% error becomes a noticeably different drink.
  • Baking — small liquid additions: extracts, citrus juice, the liqueur in a soak.

The Ounce Problem, Briefly

Two ounces of weight is 56.70 grams. Two fluid ounces of volume is 59.15 mL. Chocolate, nuts and cheese are sold by weight; milk, cream and spirits by volume. If the ingredient needs a knife to level it, the gram figure is the one you want.

A useful check: 2 fl oz of water happens to weigh 59.15 g, because water's density of 1 g/mL is built into how these units relate. Two fluid ounces of honey, though, weighs about 84 g. Same volume, different weight — that is not a technicality, it is every second recipe you will ever read.

Working Backwards From Millilitres

If your jug is marked in millilitres only, 60 mL is 2.03 fl oz — an error of 1.4%, which is fine for a cake batter and not fine for a cocktail or a setting agent like gelatine. For those, use 59.1 and round at the end.

The asymmetry is worth remembering. Recipes written in fluid ounces are almost always American, and American bakers who switch to metric tend to round the other way too — so a metric recipe asking for 60 mL and an American one asking for 2 fl oz are not quite describing the same pour.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many mL is 2 oz?

A1: 59.15 mL for 2 US fluid ounces. British and Australian recipes use the imperial fluid ounce instead, where 2 oz is 56.83 mL — roughly 4% less, enough to notice in a drink and not enough to notice in a cake.

Q2: Is 2 oz the same as a quarter cup?

A2: Yes, exactly, for US measures. A US cup holds 8 fluid ounces, so a quarter cup is 2 fluid ounces and both equal 59.15 mL. That makes the 1/4 cup measure the simplest substitute when you cannot measure fluid ounces directly.

Q3: What is 2 oz in grams?

A3: 56.70 grams, if the recipe means weight rather than volume — which is the case for solids like chocolate and nuts. Fluid ounces measure volume, not mass, so 2 fluid ounces of water weighs 59.15 g while 2 fluid ounces of honey weighs about 84 g.
