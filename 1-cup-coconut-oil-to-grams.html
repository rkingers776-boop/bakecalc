How Many Grams in 1 Cup of Coconut Oil?

1 US cup of coconut oil = 218 grams — solid or melted, the weight is identical. This is one of the few ingredients where the "solid vs liquid" question comes up constantly, and it has a satisfying answer: it doesn't matter. Melting doesn't change mass. If you scoop a cup of solid coconut oil (218g) into a pan and melt it, it still weighs 218g. The volume might look like it shrank slightly — because solid coconut oil traps tiny air pockets that melting releases — but the weight is unchanged. This is one of the strongest arguments for weighing coconut oil instead of measuring by volume. A cup of solid coconut oil, scooped from the jar, might be packed densely (230g) or airy and loose (210g) depending on how hard you pressed. Melt it and measure a cup, and you eliminate that variable entirely — 218g every time.

The Conversion Formula

  • Grams = Cups × 218
  • 1 cup solid coconut oil = 218 grams
  • 1 cup melted coconut oil = 218 grams (same!)
  • 1 Tbsp coconut oil = 13.6 grams (218 ÷ 16)

Coconut Oil vs Butter: The 9-Gram Gap

Butter weighs 227g per cup. Coconut oil weighs 218g. That's a 9-gram difference, or about 4%. For most home baking — cookies, muffins, quick breads — 9 grams won't make or break the recipe. But the real difference between butter and coconut oil isn't the 9 grams. It's the water.

Butter is about 80% fat, 15-17% water, and the rest is milk solids. When you bake with butter, that water turns to steam and creates tiny pockets of lift — this is why butter cookies have a slightly different texture than shortening cookies, and why butter pie crusts are flakier. Coconut oil is 100% fat. No water, no steam, no lift from water expansion. So when you swap coconut oil for butter 1:1 by weight in a cookie recipe, your cookies will spread slightly more (no steam lift) and be slightly denser. They'll still taste great — especially with a hint of coconut in a chocolate chip cookie, which is genuinely wonderful — but the texture will be different. A trick I use: add 1 teaspoon of water or milk per ½ cup of coconut oil when substituting for butter. It's not perfect, but it restores some of that missing steam and gets you closer to the original texture.

The Refined vs Virgin Decision

This is less about grams and more about flavor, but it's the most common coconut oil question I hear from bakers. Refined coconut oil has been processed to remove the coconut flavor and aroma. It's neutral, almost flavorless, and has a slightly higher smoke point. This is what you want 90% of the time in baking — unless you specifically want your bake to taste like coconut. Unrefined (virgin) coconut oil tastes and smells distinctly of coconut. In a coconut cake or coconut cookies, it's perfect. In a chocolate brownie, it's confusing — your brain expects chocolate and gets coconut, and the dissonance is unpleasant. I made a batch of brownies with virgin coconut oil once, thinking "coconut and chocolate go together, right?" They do — in a Bounty bar. In a brownie, it just tastes like a mistake. Use refined unless the recipe name includes the word "coconut."

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 cup of coconut oil?

A1: One US cup of coconut oil weighs 218 grams — the same whether solid or melted. Weight does not change with physical state; volume does.

Q2: Can I substitute coconut oil for butter 1:1?

A2: By weight: use 218g coconut oil for every 227g of butter called for. For most home recipes, 1:1 by volume works fine. The texture will be slightly different — less flaky, slightly more dense — because butter has water that creates steam during baking and coconut oil doesn't. For dairy-free baking, coconut oil is one of the best butter substitutes available, especially in cookies and cakes.

Q3: Should I use refined or unrefined coconut oil for baking?

A3: Refined for most baking — it has no coconut flavor and won't compete with other ingredients. Unrefined (virgin) only when you want the coconut flavor to be a deliberate part of the final product. Using virgin coconut oil in a chocolate cake will make it taste vaguely of sunscreen. Trust me on this.
