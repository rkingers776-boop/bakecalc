100 g of powdered sugar in cups — the short answer

One hundred grams of powdered sugar is 5/6 of a cup, which is 0.83 cups, or 13 tablespoons plus 1 teaspoon. It is an awkward fraction and there is a neat way around it, but first it is worth understanding why the number is awkward at all.

The 5/6 Cup Problem, and the Two-Scoop Fix

No measuring cup set made for home kitchens contains a 5/6 cup. The fractions you get are 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, and 1.

The fix is that 5/6 is the sum of two of them: 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6. So fill the half cup, fill the third cup, and tip both into the bowl. Compared with trying to eyeball five sixths of a single measure, it is both easier and more accurate, and it needs nothing you do not already own.

If you would rather spoon it: 13 level tablespoons is 97.5 g and one more teaspoon takes it to 100 g exactly.

Sifting Makes It Exactly One Cup

This is the part of powdered sugar measurement that catches people out. The same 100 grams fills a measurable fifth more space once it has passed through a sieve:

  • 100 g, straight from the bag — 5/6 cup, at roughly 120 g per cup
  • 100 g, sifted — 1 cup exactly, at roughly 100 g per cup

Sifting does not remove anything or compress anything. It separates the clumps that settled in the bag and coats every particle in a film of air, and that air is volume. A recipe that asks for one cup of sifted powdered sugar and gets an unsifted cup instead has been given about 120 g — a fifth more sugar than intended, along with a fifth more cornstarch.

How Much Cornstarch 100 g Carries

Powdered sugar is about 3 to 5% cornstarch by weight, which puts a 100 g portion at somewhere around 3 to 5 g of starch. That is a little over a teaspoon, and it is not a rounding detail. Cornstarch thickens any liquid it is heated in, so icing made from 100 g of powdered sugar will set a shade firmer than the same icing made from powdered sugar without it. It is also the reason nobody can substitute a blender full of granulated sugar and expect the same result.

What 100 g of Powdered Sugar Makes

  • A pourable glaze — 100 g with 2 tablespoons of milk, for a bundt or a loaf.
  • A spreadable icing — 100 g with 1 tablespoon of milk, plus a pinch of salt to cut the sweetness.
  • Small-batch royal icing — 100 g with one egg white, enough to outline and flood a tray of cookies.
  • Buttercream for one thin layer — 100 g with 50 g of soft butter, enough to fill a single 8 inch round.

Notice that all of these are about texture rather than sweetness. Powdered sugar's job in icing is to build structure out of dissolved solids, and the 3 to 5 g of cornstarch is doing some of that work.

Scooping Versus Weighing — Where the 20 g Goes

Powdered sugar is one of the most compressible ingredients in the pantry, closer to flour than to its own granulated parent. Two people measuring one cup can land 20 g apart without either of them doing anything wrong:

  • Spooned and levelled — about 110 g per cup
  • Scooped and levelled — about 120 g per cup
  • Scooped and tapped down — 135 g or more

On a 100 g portion that spread is the difference between a glaze that pours and one that sits in the bowl. If the icing texture is the point of the recipe, weigh it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 100g of powdered sugar?

A1: 5/6 of a cup, or 0.83 cups, which is 13 tablespoons plus 1 teaspoon. Unsifted powdered sugar is taken as 120 grams per cup.

Q2: How do I measure 5/6 cup without a 5/6 cup measure?

A2: Use a half cup plus a third cup. Those two add up to five sixths exactly, and both measures come in every standard set — which is why this is the practical way to hit 100 grams.

Q3: Does sifting change how many cups 100g is?

A3: Yes, by a sixth. Unsifted, 100 grams fills 5/6 of a cup. Sifted, it fills a full cup, because sifting lifts the density from about 120 grams per cup down to about 100.
