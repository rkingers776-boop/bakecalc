About BakeCalc Club

Free baking conversion tools — built by a baker who got tired of googling "how many grams in a cup of flour" three times a week.

Why This Site Exists

I bake a lot. I also collect recipes from everywhere — American blogs, British cookbooks, French patisserie books, Japanese YouTube channels. Every single one uses different units. Cups, grams, ounces, milliliters, tablespoons. Converting back and forth meant opening a dozen browser tabs and hoping the numbers were right.

The problem with most conversion sites: they use one generic number for everything. A cup of flour is not the same weight as a cup of sugar or a cup of cocoa powder. A tablespoon of butter weighs more than a tablespoon of oil. If you use the wrong number, your dough is too dry or too wet. In baking, that's the difference between a cake and a brick.

So I built BakeCalc Club — a set of calculators that know the difference between every ingredient. All-purpose flour is 120g per cup. Cocoa powder is 100g. Butter is 227g. Blanched almond flour is 96g. Each one gets its own page, its own density, its own calculator, and the same number everywhere on the site. No generic averages, no guesswork.

How We Choose the Numbers

There is no single correct weight for a cup of flour. That is not a gap in our tables — it is a fact about measuring by volume, and every conversion site has to take a position on it.

A US cup holds 236.6 mL no matter what you put in it. What changes is the weight, because flour, sugar and cocoa pack differently. That is why we publish a page per ingredient instead of one generic table: the number you get should be the number for the thing actually in your cup.

The bigger variable is how the cup got filled. Spooned in and levelled, all-purpose flour weighs about 120 g. Scooped straight from the bag and levelled — which compresses it — the same cup can weigh 150 g. That is a 25% spread, and it is the most common single reason a cake comes out dry or dense. Our all-purpose flour is 120 g per cup, because that matches the spoon-and-level method most US recipes are written against.

Other tables list 125 g or 150 g for the same cup. Those are not wrong; they are answers to a slightly different question. Where a credible source disagrees with us by more than a few percent, we say so on that ingredient's own page rather than quietly averaging the difference away.

The Reference List

These are the numbers the entire site uses. If you find a page on BakeCalc Club that contradicts this list, that is a bug — tell us and we will fix it.

  • All-purpose flour — 120 g/cup (bread flour 130 g, cake flour 110 g, whole wheat 120 g)
  • Granulated sugar — 200 g/cup · packed brown sugar — 220 g/cup · powdered sugar — 120 g/cup (100 g sifted)
  • Butter — 227 g/cup (2 US sticks) · vegetable oil — 218 g/cup · coconut oil — 218 g/cup
  • Cocoa powder — 100 g/cup (85 g sifted) · cornstarch — 128 g/cup · rolled oats — 100 g/cup
  • Instant yeast — 3.1 g/tsp · baking powder — 4.6 g/tsp
  • 1 large egg — 50 g shelled (about 30 g white, 18 g yolk)
