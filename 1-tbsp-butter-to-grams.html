1 Tbsp Butter to Grams Converter

Convert a tablespoon of butter into grams — 14.2 — and see why American butter wrappers make this the easiest fat in the kitchen to measure.

Gram Equivalent:

14.2 grams (14.2 g)

*1 cup unsalted butter = 227g, 1 tbsp = 1/16 cup = 14.2g. Salted ≈ 14.3g/tbsp, European-style ≈ 14.1g/tbsp.

1 tablespoon of butter in grams — the short answer

One tablespoon of butter is 14.2 grams. The chain of reasoning is short and entirely about packaging: a US stick of butter is a quarter of a pound, a quarter pound is 113.4 g, a stick is eight tablespoons, and 113.4 divided by 8 is 14.175.

Nothing about that is a convention. It is what a quarter pound comes to when you divide it.

The Wrapper Is the Measuring Tool

Butter is the only common ingredient whose packaging is designed to make measuring instruments unnecessary. American butter is sold in quarter-pound sticks wrapped in paper printed with tablespoon marks, so a recipe calling for three tablespoons is satisfied by cutting at the third line.

The marks exist because the arithmetic works out. Because a stick is 8 tablespoons exactly, every whole tablespoon is an eighth of the stick, and halves and quarters follow. Nothing needs to be scooped, levelled, or weighed.

The alternative — pressing soft butter into a tablespoon measure — is worse than it sounds. Soft butter traps air, the spoon fills unevenly, and the same spoonful can weigh 12 g or 17 g depending on how firmly it was pushed in. Which is why the wrapper exists.

A Metric Block Has No Such Marks

Outside the United States, butter usually comes as a single block — commonly 250 g, sometimes 200 g or 500 g — with no tablespoon divisions and often no markings beyond the total weight.

That is the situation in which 14.2 g becomes genuinely useful. The bridge from a metric block to an American recipe is the number: cut or spoon 14.2 g per tablespoon, and the recipe's internal ratios stay intact. The 250 g block conveniently contains 17.6 tablespoons.

Tablespoons, Sticks, and Cups

  • 1 tbsp — 14.2 g, 1/8 stick
  • 2 tbsp — 28.4 g, 1/4 stick
  • 4 tbsp — 56.7 g, 1/2 stick, 1/4 cup
  • 8 tbsp — 113.4 g, 1 stick, 1/2 cup
  • 12 tbsp — 170 g, 1 1/2 sticks, 3/4 cup
  • 16 tbsp — 227 g, 2 sticks, 1 cup

The site's converter uses 227 g per cup, which is the round figure for two sticks. The precise value is 226.8 g, and no recipe will notice the difference.

Salted, Unsalted, and European at a Tablespoon

All three weigh the same per tablespoon, and all three behave slightly differently.

  • Unsalted butter — the default for baking, because it lets the recipe control the salt. About 80% butterfat.
  • Salted butter — salt is added at roughly 1.5 to 2% of the weight, which puts a tablespoon at about 90 mg of sodium. In a recipe using eight tablespoons that is 720 mg, which is enough to make a sweet bake taste seasoned.
  • European-style butter — usually 82% butterfat or higher, because it is churned longer and contains less water. A tablespoon still weighs 14.2 g, but it carries marginally more fat and less water, which reads as a slightly flakier pastry and a slightly richer crumb.

The salt in salted butter also makes it tenderise differently in pastry: salt interferes with gluten formation, so an all-salted-butter pie crust can be a touch shorter than the same crust made with unsalted butter plus added salt.

Melting Changes the Volume, Not the Weight

A tablespoon of solid butter is 14.2 g. Melt it and it is still 14.2 g, because melting removes nothing. What changes is the volume occupied.

Solid butter contains air and a crystalline fat structure that holds it in place. Melted butter has neither, so a given weight of it takes up less room than the same weight did in the solid state. The practical figures are the ones worth remembering:

  • A cup of solid butter — 227 g
  • A cup of melted butter — about 240 g, because the melt fills the gaps the solid left

Which is why the wording of a recipe matters. "Half a cup of butter, melted" means measure solid and then melt. "Half a cup of melted butter" means melt first and measure the liquid, which is about 5% more butter by weight.

Butter Against Oil at a Tablespoon

Both fats measure easily and they are not equivalent, because butter is not pure fat.

  • 1 tbsp butter — 14.2 g, of which about 11.4 g is fat, 2.4 g is water, and 0.4 g is milk solids
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil — 13.6 g, all of it fat

So a spoon of oil carries about 19% more fat than a spoon of butter, despite weighing slightly less. Replacing a tablespoon of butter with oil means using about 2 1/2 teaspoons, not a full tablespoon. Working the other way, replacing a tablespoon of oil with butter takes about 1 1/4 tablespoons.

The water is worth tracking at scale. Eight tablespoons of butter carries around 20 g of water; swap in oil and the recipe loses nearly three tablespoons of liquid, which is enough to change a batter's consistency.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 tablespoon of butter?

A1: 14.2 grams. A US stick of butter is a quarter pound, or 113.4 grams, and it divides into eight tablespoons, so each one is 14.175 grams.

Q2: How many tablespoons are in a stick of butter?

A2: Eight. The wrapper is printed with a mark for each one, so a recipe asking for three tablespoons can be measured by cutting the paper rather than using a spoon.

Q3: Is a tablespoon of butter the same as a tablespoon of oil?

A3: Not in fat terms. A tablespoon of butter weighs more but is only about 80% fat, so it delivers around 11.4 grams of fat against the oil's 13.6.
