How Many Grams in 1 Cup of Almond Flour?

1 US cup of blanched super-fine almond flour = about 105 grams. Now, this number is not as firm as it is for wheat flour (120g) or sugar (200g) because almond flour varies wildly by brand. Bob's Red Mill super-fine clocks in around 100–105g per cup when spooned lightly. King Arthur's almond flour is similar. Store-brand almond flour that's coarser can hit 115g. Almond meal (skins on, coarser grind) is typically 110–115g. Compare all of this to wheat flour at 120g/cup — almond flour is lighter because it's roughly 50% fat, and fat is less dense than starch. This matters when you're converting a gluten-free recipe designed for almond flour: the recipe developer tested with a specific brand, and your brand might differ by 10g per cup. Weigh it.

The Conversion Formula

  • Grams = Cups × Grams-per-Cup
  • Super-fine blanched: 1 cup × 105 = 105 grams
  • Lightly spooned (any brand): 1 cup × 100 = ~100 grams
  • Almond meal (skins on): 1 cup × 112 = ~112 grams

Gluten-Free Baking: Why Almond Flour Is Different

Wheat flour is mostly starch and protein (gluten). When you add water and mix, gluten forms a stretchy network that traps gas and gives structure. Almond flour has zero gluten, zero starch — it's basically ground nuts. Almost all of its weight is fat and protein, and neither of those builds structure the way gluten does. This is why almond flour recipes almost always call for extra eggs: the egg proteins set during baking and create a fragile scaffolding that replaces what gluten would have done. It's also why almond flour baked goods are tender and moist (all that fat) but crumbly and fragile (no gluten network). You can't pick up a slice of almond flour cake with one hand the way you can with a wheat cake — it'll fall apart. That's not a bug. It's the nature of the ingredient.

One thing I wish I'd known before my first almond flour baking experiment: almond flour burns faster than wheat flour. All those almond fats start toasting in the oven at a lower temperature than wheat starch browns. If your almond flour cake recipe says 350°F, don't push it to 375°F to save time. You'll get a dark, bitter crust that tastes like burnt nuts — not in a good way. I made this mistake with a batch of macarons that came out looking like tiny hockey pucks. Tasted like them too.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 cup of almond flour?

A1: About 100–112 grams, depending on the grind and whether skins are included. Super-fine blanched almond flour (most common for baking) is about 105g per cup when spooned. Coarser almond meal with skins is about 110–115g.

Q2: Why is almond flour lighter than regular flour per cup?

A2: Almond flour is about 50% fat, and fat is lighter than starch per unit of volume. Wheat flour (120g/cup) is mostly starch with some protein. Almond flour's fat content also makes it clump more easily, which is why you should always spoon and level — never scoop.

Q3: Can I use almond meal instead of almond flour in a recipe?

A3: It depends on the recipe. For macarons, financiers, and delicate cakes: no — the skins and coarse texture of almond meal will ruin the texture. For rustic cakes, crusts, and cookies: yes, almond meal works well, and the skins add a pleasant nuttiness. The weight difference is small (5-10g per cup), but the texture difference is significant.

Q4: Can I replace wheat flour with almond flour in my favorite recipe?

A4: Not 1:1. Almond flour has no gluten and is mostly fat — a cake made entirely with almond flour will be dense and fragile without additional binders (usually eggs). A safe starting point: replace up to 25% of the wheat flour with almond flour by weight and see how it goes. For fully gluten-free baking, use a recipe developed specifically for almond flour — don't try to convert a wheat recipe yourself, at least not the first time.
