Convert tablespoons of coconut oil to grams — solid vs. melted, refined vs. unrefined, and when 0.2 grams actually makes a difference.

1 Tablespoon Coconut Oil = 13.6 Grams. Here Is When That Number Earns Its Keep.

Coconut oil at the tablespoon level is a deceptively simple ingredient. One US tablespoon of solid coconut oil weighs about 13.6 grams. Melt it and you get about 13.4 grams per tablespoon — a 0.2-gram difference that is below the resolution of most kitchen scales. But the real measurement challenge is not the solid-to-liquid density shift. It is the air pockets. Solid coconut oil scooped from a jar at room temperature traps air like cold butter — the irregular surface of each scoop leaves gaps that the oil does not fill. A "level tablespoon" of solid coconut oil can easily range from 12 grams (lightly scooped) to 15 grams (firmly packed), which is a 25% variation range around the true 13.6-gram value. Melted coconut oil pours smoothly and fills the tablespoon with no air gaps, making it more consistent by volume — but only if the recipe developer measured it melted in the first place. This is the quiet argument for weighing coconut oil in grams regardless of quantity. A tablespoon is small enough that a 1-2 gram error might not ruin a whole recipe, but in a dressing that uses only 2 tablespoons of oil total, a 4-gram error is a 15% fat swing — enough to throw off the emulsion balance between oil and acid.

Notice something interesting: coconut oil and vegetable oil are essentially identical in per-tablespoon weight (13.6g). Butter is about 4% heavier. This means that if you are substituting coconut oil for butter by the tablespoon in a pan sauce or saute, the weight difference is trivial — but the fat-to-water ratio is not. Butter's water content (about 2.3g of water per tablespoon) steams off during cooking; coconut oil's 100% fat content stays in the pan. The result is a slightly richer finish with coconut oil, even though the starting weights are nearly the same. Our tablespoon butter guide breaks down the butter side of the equation.

Solid vs. Melted at the Tablespoon Scale: Why It (Almost) Does Not Matter

Coconut oil's phase change — solid below roughly 76 degrees Fahrenheit, liquid above it — is fascinating from a physics perspective but nearly irrelevant from a weight perspective at the tablespoon level. The density of coconut oil changes by about 1.4% between solid and liquid states, which works out to 0.2 grams per tablespoon. Your kitchen scale probably reports in 1-gram increments, which means it literally cannot see the difference. The real variable is whether the solid oil is packed into the tablespoon with air gaps or melted and poured to fill the spoon completely. A firmly packed tablespoon of solid coconut oil can weigh 15 grams; a loosely scooped one can weigh 12 grams. Melt and pour that same tablespoon and you will get a consistent 13.4 to 13.6 grams every time. The phase change matters for measurement consistency, not for actual density change. If precision at the tablespoon level matters for your recipe — and it might, if you are making something finicky like a coconut-oil-based pie crust in a half batch — melt the oil first and weigh it. Refined vs. Unrefined: Pick for Flavor, Not for Weight

Refined coconut oil (steam-deodorized, neutral in flavor and aroma) and unrefined/virgin coconut oil (coconut-scented and flavored) weigh the same at the tablespoon level: 13.6 grams. The refining process strips out volatile aromatic compounds measured in parts per million — nothing that moves a gram scale. Choose refined coconut oil when you want the functional benefits of coconut oil (100% fat, high smoke point, solid-at-room-temperature structure) without your baked goods tasting like suntan lotion. Choose unrefined when you are making something where coconut flavor is a welcome guest: granola, curry, coconut cake, tropical smoothies. The weight is the same either way. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Does solid coconut oil weigh the same as melted at the tablespoon level?

A1: For all practical purposes, yes. Solid coconut oil ≈ 13.6g per tablespoon; melted ≈ 13.4g per tablespoon. The 0.2-gram difference is smaller than the 1-gram resolution of most kitchen scales. The bigger source of error is how the solid oil is packed into the spoon — a loose scoop versus a firmly packed tablespoon can vary by 2-3 grams, which dwarfs the true density difference between phases.

Q2: How does a tablespoon of coconut oil compare to a tablespoon of butter?

A2: Coconut oil: 13.6g per tablespoon. Butter: 14.2g per tablespoon. The 0.6g difference (about 4%) is small enough for casual substitution, but the composition difference is bigger than the weight difference: coconut oil is 100% fat, while butter is about 80% fat with 20% water and milk solids. Swapping one for the other changes the fat-to-water ratio in your recipe even when the gram weights are close.

Q3: What temperature should coconut oil be when measuring in tablespoons?

A3: For most recipes, room temperature (below 76°F / 24°C, where coconut oil is solid) is standard — scoop and level like shortening. If the recipe says "coconut oil, melted," melt it first and then measure the liquid. The instruction matters because solid coconut oil scoops with air pockets that can cause 1-2 grams of variation per tablespoon; melted oil pours consistently and fills the spoon completely. When in doubt, melt and weigh — it is the most reproducible approach.
