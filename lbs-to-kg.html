Pounds to Kilograms — Because Not Everyone Shops at Kroger

Convert pounds (lbs) to kilograms (kg) for recipes, grocery shopping, and bulk buying. The conversion is simple — knowing when to use it is the real skill.

Quick:

Weight in Kilograms & Grams:

0.45 kg (454 g)

1 lb = 0.45359237 kg. The calculator uses this exact conversion — just rounded for display.

The Farmers Market Problem

I live in a country that uses metric but my brain was raised on pounds. At the farmers market, the beef guy sells in kilograms. The apple lady sells in kilograms. The flour mill sells in kilograms. But my grandmother's recipes — the ones I actually cook from — are all in pounds. "Three pounds of chuck roast." "A pound and a half of apples." "Five pounds of bread flour."

I got tired of doing the math on my phone while holding a bag of apples in the other hand, so here's the shortcut I now use: divide pounds by 2.2 to get kilograms. Multiply kilograms by 2.2 to get pounds. It's not perfectly exact (the real factor is 2.20462) but it's close enough for everything except pharmaceutical dosing. Nobody's ever complained that my 3-pound roast was actually 2.98 pounds.

Pounds, Stone, and the UK's Awkward Transition

Britain is in a weird middle ground: legally metric since 1995, but culturally still imperial for many things. A British recipe might say "1.5 kg flour" while a British person at a pub will tell you they weigh "12 stone." (A stone is 14 pounds, by the way — 12 stone = 168 pounds = 76.2 kg.)

If you're converting a recipe from a British source, check whether the weights look like round metric numbers (500g, 1kg, 250g) or round imperial numbers (1 lb, 8 oz, 2 lb). Round metric numbers mean the author intended metric. Round imperial numbers mean the metric on the label is a conversion and might be slightly off from the author's real intention. Scale accordingly.

The ÷2.2 Mental Math Shortcut

For 90% of cooking purposes, dividing by 2.2 is accurate enough. Examples:

  • 5 lbs ÷ 2.2 = 2.27 kg (exact: 2.268 kg — off by 0.1%)
  • 10 lbs ÷ 2.2 = 4.55 kg (exact: 4.536 kg — off by 0.3%)
  • 1 lb ÷ 2.2 = 0.455 kg = 455 g (exact: 454 g — off by 1 g in a pound)

If you need more precision, use the converter at the top of this page. If you're standing in the grocery store trying to figure out if that 2.5 kg bag of flour is enough for a recipe that calls for 5 pounds: 2.5 × 2.2 = 5.5 lbs. Yes, you're fine. You even have half a pound extra. This is the only kind of kitchen math that actually matters in real life.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert pounds to kilograms?

Multiply pounds by 0.453592. For quick estimates, divide pounds by 2.2. Example: 10 lbs × 0.4536 = 4.54 kg. To go from kg to lbs: multiply kg by 2.2046.

Why does the US still use pounds?

US metrication in the 1970s was voluntary, not mandatory. Without a legal requirement, consumers had no reason to switch, and manufacturers followed demand. Most US packaged foods show both pounds and grams — check the label.

How do I convert pounds to grams directly?

Multiply pounds by 453.592. 1 lb = 454 g (rounded). For ounces to grams: 1 oz = 28.35 g. Use our oz to grams converter for smaller amounts.
