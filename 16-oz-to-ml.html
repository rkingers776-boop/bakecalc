16 oz in mL — the short answer

Sixteen US fluid ounces is 473.18 mL — 16 × 29.5735 = 473.1765. That is one US pint, exactly, by definition. But this is the point in the ounce scale where a straightforward conversion stops being straightforward, because the word pint means something different on the other side of the Atlantic.

Sixteen Ounces Is a Pint — in America

The US pint is defined as 16 fluid ounces, which is 473.18 mL, which is 2 cups. Everything above it follows by doubling: a quart is 32 fl oz, a gallon is 128. Once you have the pint, the rest of the system is arithmetic.

In the kitchen, the useful equivalence is two cups. Cup measures exist in pairs in most drawers, so a pint of stock or milk is easy to build without a pint jug.

Why a British Pint Holds 20% More

In Britain and Ireland, a pint is 20 imperial fluid ounces, which is 568.26 mL. Ask for a pint of beer on either side of the Atlantic and you get a difference of 95 mL — close to a fifth of the glass. That is not a rounding difference; it is a visible extra mouthful and a half.

The split starts at the gallon, not the pint. The US kept the wine gallon of 1707 — 231 cubic inches — when it inherited English measures. Britain replaced its own in 1824 with a gallon defined as the volume of ten pounds of distilled water at a specified temperature, about 277.42 cubic inches. Every smaller unit divided downward from a different gallon, so pints, quarts and fluid ounces all drifted apart together.

  • US fluid ounce — 29.5735 mL. A US pint is 16 of them: 473.18 mL.
  • Imperial fluid ounce — 28.4131 mL. An imperial pint is 20 of them: 568.26 mL.

Note that the imperial fluid ounce is the smaller of the two, which is why the larger pint is not a simple multiple. A British pint contains 20 small ounces against the American 16 large ones, and the count wins.

Where 16 oz Shows Up

  • Iced coffee and cold brew — 16 oz is the standard large cup, and the reason the figure gets searched.
  • Beer cans — the 16 oz can, sold as a tallboy, is a mainstay of American craft brewing.
  • Ice cream — a pint container is 473 mL by volume, though the label usually states a weight of 14 to 16 oz.
  • Yogurt and cottage cheese — 16 oz tubs, labelled by weight.

Sixteen Ounces by Weight Is a Pound

Sixteen ounces of weight is 453.59 g, which is one pound. The coincidence is neat and a little cruel: 16 oz by volume is a pint, and 16 oz by weight is a pound, so the same four characters describe two unrelated quantities that both happen to land on whole units.

Ice cream shows how the two interact in real packaging. A pint of ice cream is 473 mL of volume, but it is sold by weight, and weights of 14 to 16 oz are common for the same nominal pint — because overrun, the air churned into the mix, varies by brand. A container labelled 16 oz may well hold more than a pint.

Two Cups, or Half a Quart

The kitchen equivalents for 16 fl oz are 2 cups and half a quart. Pick one and stay with it through a recipe rather than converting back and forth; the errors people make with this figure come from switching systems mid-formula, not from the arithmetic itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many mL is 16 oz?

A1: 473.18 mL for 16 US fluid ounces, which is exactly one US pint. Under the imperial system the same words describe 20 fluid ounces, or 568.26 mL.

Q2: Is a British pint the same as an American pint?

A2: No. A US pint is 16 US fluid ounces, 473.18 mL. A British or Irish pint is 20 imperial fluid ounces, 568.26 mL — about 20% larger. The difference traces back to the two countries defining their gallons differently in 1824.

Q3: How many cups is 16 oz?

A3: Two cups, for a liquid. A US cup is 8 fluid ounces, so 16 fluid ounces is exactly 2 cups, or half a quart. Sixteen ounces of a solid by weight is 454 grams, and its volume depends entirely on the ingredient.
