How Many Grams in 1 Teaspoon of Baking Powder?

1 US teaspoon of baking powder equals 4.6 grams. Baking soda is the same — 4.6g per teaspoon. These two are the most weight-sensitive ingredients in baking, and a small measuring error has an outsized effect on your results.

Why Leaveners Deserve a Scale

If you only weigh one category of ingredient, make it leavening agents. Here's why:

  • Small absolute amounts, large relative errors. A recipe might call for 1 teaspoon of baking powder (4.6g). A heaping vs level teaspoon can differ by 1–2 grams — that's a 20–40% error on the most chemically active ingredient in your batter.
  • Baking powder is time-sensitive. Once opened, baking powder absorbs moisture from the air and loses potency over 6–12 months. Weighing it doesn't fix old baking powder, but it ensures you're at least using the amount the recipe intends — one less variable to debug.
  • High-altitude adjustments are easier in grams. Above 3,000 feet, you typically reduce baking powder by 15–20%. 1 tsp is 4.6g; 15% less is 3.9g. Much easier to weigh 3.9g than to eyeball "a scant teaspoon."

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 teaspoon of baking powder?

A1: One US teaspoon of baking powder weighs 4.6 grams. This is the standard conversion used by professional bakers and recipe developers worldwide.

Q2: Is baking soda the same weight as baking powder per teaspoon?

A2: Yes — both weigh approximately 4.6 grams per US teaspoon. Despite being chemically different (baking soda is pure sodium bicarbonate; baking powder contains sodium bicarbonate plus acid), their densities by volume are nearly identical.

Q3: Why should I weigh baking powder instead of using measuring spoons?

A3: Leavening agents are the most measurement-sensitive ingredients in baking. A heaped vs level teaspoon can differ by 1-2 grams — a 20-40% error. That's enough to turn a fluffy cake into a dense brick. Weighing eliminates this variable entirely.
