1/2 cup of flour in grams — the short answer

Half a cup of all-purpose flour is 60 grams, measured by spooning the flour into the cup and levelling it off. That is exactly half the 120 g a full cup weighs by the same method, which makes this one of the cleanest conversions in the kitchen.

It is also, for the same reason, one of the most deceptive. The 120 g baseline only holds for one filling technique, and half a cup inherits whatever inaccuracy the technique brings with it.

The Same Half Cup, Two Different Weights

Flour compacts. Scoop the measuring cup straight into the bag and the walls press the flour against itself as it fills, so the same volume holds more. The effect scales down with the cup but does not disappear:

  • Spooned and levelled — 60 g for half a cup, on the 120 g per cup standard
  • Scooped and swept — about 72 g, on a scooped cup of 145 g
  • Sifted, then spooned — closer to 50 g, because sifting works air into the flour

Between the lightest and the heaviest reading there is 22 g, and on a 60 g quantity that is a 37% swing. In a cookie dough it will pass unnoticed. In a sponge, a macaron or a small batch of anything where the flour-to-liquid balance is tight, it is the difference between a batter that works and one that does not.

Why Half a Cup Is the Most-Used Fraction

Half a cup sits at an unusual intersection: it is small enough to appear in almost every recipe and large enough to be measured with a single tool. Most American cup sets include a dedicated half-cup measure, so unlike a third or three quarters, it never has to be assembled from two scoops.

That convenience is part of the problem. A measure that is always used casually is a measure that is always approximate, and half a cup of flour is the quantity most likely to be estimated without anyone noticing.

Where Half a Cup of Flour Shows Up

Half a cup is the flour quantity in a single six-inch cake layer, a small batch of four muffins, a dozen small cookies, and a pan sauce thickener for a large dish. It appears in these roles so often that most home bakers develop a feel for it — which is precisely why it gets measured by eye and why the 22-gram spread goes unnoticed until something fails.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1/2 cup of flour?

A1: 60 grams of all-purpose flour, spooned into the cup and levelled off. That is exactly half of the 120 grams a full cup weighs by the same method, so the fraction is clean.

Q2: Why does 1/2 cup of flour sometimes weigh more?

A2: Because a scooped cup weighs 145 grams or more rather than 120. Half of a scooped cup is about 72 grams, so the same half cup can be 60 or 72 depending on how it was filled — a 20% difference that no recipe can absorb in a delicate bake.

Q3: Is half a cup of flour the same as 4 tablespoons?

A3: No — half a cup is 8 tablespoons. A full cup is 16 tablespoons, so half is 8. Four tablespoons would be a quarter cup, which is 30 grams of flour.
