How Many Grams in 1/2 Cup of Flour?

The half-cup is the most-used fraction in home baking — and ½ cup of all-purpose flour equals 60 grams. That's exactly half of the 120-gram full-cup standard. But if you're working with bread flour or cake flour, that 60 g number shifts: bread flour is denser at ~65 g per half cup, and cake flour is lighter at ~55 g per half cup.

The Conversion Formula (Cups → Grams)

For any flour type, the math is simply half the full-cup density:

  • Grams = Cups × Grams-per-Cup
  • All-Purpose Flour: 0.5 × 120 g = 60 grams
  • Bread Flour: 0.5 × 130 g = 65 grams
  • Cake Flour: 0.5 × 110 g = 55 grams

Why Half-Cup Accuracy Matters

Many small-batch recipes — mug cakes, cookies for two, single-layer cakes — use ½ cup increments. A 5-gram error on a full cup is minor; on a half cup it's proportionally twice as impactful. With flour, that 5-gram difference between all-purpose (60 g) and bread flour (65 g) per half cup can mean the difference between a tender crumb and a tough one in a small-batch recipe.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1/2 cup of all-purpose flour?

A1: One half cup (½ US cup) of all-purpose flour weighs approximately 60 grams — exactly half of the standard 120 g full-cup baseline used in professional baking.

Q2: Is 1/2 cup of bread flour heavier than all-purpose?

A2: Yes — ½ cup of bread flour weighs about 65 grams versus 60 g for all-purpose, due to higher protein density. Cake flour is lighter at roughly 55 g per half cup.

Q3: Can I use a 1/2 cup measure for all flour types equally?

A3: Volume-wise yes, but weight differs: all-purpose ≈ 60 g, bread ≈ 65 g, cake ≈ 55 g. If your recipe uses weights, match the flour type — don't assume a single gram value for all flours.
