How to Accurately Measure 150 Grams of Brown Sugar

Brown sugar is a staple in cookies, muffins, and barbecue rubs. Because packed brown sugar contains molasses and has a higher moisture content, its density varies significantly from white or powdered sugars. At 220 grams per packed cup, 150 grams of brown sugar works out to roughly 2/3 of a standard US measuring cup.

The Mathematical Conversion Formula

The conversion uses the standard packed brown sugar density of 220g per cup:

  • Total Cup Volume = Given Weight in Grams ÷ Standard Brown Sugar Weight Per Cup
  • Packed Brown: 150g ÷ 220g/cup = 0.68 cups (roughly 2/3 cup)
  • Granulated: 150g ÷ 200g/cup = 0.75 cups (3/4 cup)
  • Powdered: 150g ÷ 120g/cup = 1.25 cups (1 1/4 cups)

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 150g of packed brown sugar?

A1: 150 grams of packed brown sugar equals roughly 0.68 cups, or about 2/3 cup. Packed brown sugar is denser than white sugar at 220g per cup.

Q2: What is the difference between light and dark brown sugar for 150g conversions?

A2: Light and dark brown sugar have essentially the same density when packed, so 150g of either type converts to roughly 2/3 cup. The difference is only in molasses content and flavor intensity, not weight.

Q3: How does the moisture in brown sugar affect my 150g measurement?

A3: Brown sugar's moisture content can cause it to clump, which affects volume measurements. For the most accurate 150g portion, always use a digital kitchen scale. If measuring by cup, firmly pack the sugar into the cup for consistency.
