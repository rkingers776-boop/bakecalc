150g Brown Sugar to Cups Converter

Convert 150 grams of brown sugar into cups, and see how much molasses that weight actually carries.

Cup Equivalent:

Roughly 2/3 cup (0.68 cups)

*Calculated using standard US customary culinary measuring cups for packed brown sugar.

150 g of brown sugar in cups — the short answer

One hundred and fifty grams of packed brown sugar is 0.68 cups, which is two thirds of a cup, or about 11 level tablespoons. Two thirds of a cup packed weighs 146.7 g, so 150 g is that plus a slightly rounded teaspoon. At 220 grams per cup, the arithmetic is that short.

Where 150 g Falls Between the Fractions

Brown sugar quantities tend to arrive at the round fractions, and 150 g is one of the few weights that lands on a clean one. The useful landmarks:

  • 1/2 cup packed — 110 g
  • 2/3 cup packed — 147 g, which is what 150 g rounds to
  • 3/4 cup packed — 165 g
  • 1 cup packed — 220 g

So a recipe that calls for two thirds of a cup of packed brown sugar is asking for 150 g, whether or not it says so. That equivalence is worth holding on to, because it means you can follow an American volume recipe with a scale and skip the packing entirely.

The Molasses Inside 150 g

Brown sugar is granulated sugar plus molasses, and the percentages are smaller than the flavour suggests. Light brown runs about 3.5% molasses, dark about 6.5%.

On 150 g that works out as:

  • Light brown sugar — roughly 5 g of molasses, a little under a teaspoon
  • Dark brown sugar — roughly 10 g, about a teaspoon and a half

Five grams is two or three percent of the total weight, and it is carrying the entire flavour difference between brown and white sugar. It is also the reason the popular home substitution — a tablespoon of molasses stirred into a cup of granulated sugar — overshoots. A tablespoon of molasses weighs about 20 g, which on a 220 g cup is closer to 9% and produces something distinctly darker than the light brown sugar it is meant to imitate.

Swapping 150 g of Brown Sugar for Granulated

If you are out of brown sugar, the substitution is straightforward but not weight-for-weight, because the two sugars have different densities and different water content.

  • By weight — 150 g of granulated sugar plus about 1 teaspoon of molasses, beaten together until even in colour.
  • By volume — 2/3 cup of granulated sugar measures 133 g, which is 17 g short of the brown sugar it replaces. Add the teaspoon of molasses and you are close enough.
  • What changes — the acidity drops and the moisture drops, so a recipe relying on brown sugar for chew will come out crisper.

Packed Against Loose at 150 g

The volume you need depends entirely on how you fill the cup, and the gap widens with the amount:

  • Packed firmly — 0.68 cups, or two thirds
  • Spooned and levelled — 0.83 cups, or five sixths
  • Poured from the bag — around 0.94 cups

Five sixths of a cup has the same advantage as the powdered sugar case: 1/2 + 1/3 adds up to 5/6 exactly, so it is two measures rather than an estimate.

What 150 g of Brown Sugar Bakes

  • A dozen cookies — 150 g of brown sugar to 115 g of butter is a workable small batch, with the brown sugar doing the chewy work.
  • A loaf cake or quick bread — 150 g as the total sweetener gives a soft crumb and a caramel edge.
  • A banana bread — where the sugar's acidity and moisture both help, and the fruit supplies the rest of the sweetness.
  • A glaze or a caramel sauce — 150 g with 50 g of butter and a splash of cream, cooked until it coats a spoon.

Storing It So 150 g Measures as 150 g

Hardened brown sugar is not spoiled, it is merely dry, but it does measure differently: a brick crumbles into irregular pieces that leave air gaps in the cup, so the same volume weighs less. Two fixes, both safe:

  • A slice of bread or half an apple in an airtight container for a day. The sugar draws the moisture across.
  • Short bursts in the microwave with a damp paper towel, 15 seconds at a time, breaking up the lumps between bursts.

Neither restores the original texture exactly, but both restore packability, which is what the conversion depends on.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 150g of brown sugar?

A1: 0.68 cups when packed, which is two thirds of a cup, or about 11 level tablespoons. The figure assumes 220 grams per packed cup.

Q2: How much molasses is in 150g of brown sugar?

A2: About 5 grams in a light brown sugar, a little under a teaspoon, or roughly 10 grams in a dark brown. Molasses makes up only a few percent of the weight even though it dominates the flavour.

Q3: Can I make 150g of brown sugar from granulated sugar?

A3: Yes. Take 150 grams of granulated sugar and add molasses to taste — around a teaspoon for a light brown result. The widely quoted one-tablespoon-per-cup rule is generous and lands closer to dark brown than light.
