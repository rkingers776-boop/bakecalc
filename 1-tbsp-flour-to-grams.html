*Calculated using 1 cup flour = 120g, 1 tbsp = 1/16 cup, spoon-and-level method.

1 tablespoon of flour in grams — the short answer

One level tablespoon of all-purpose flour is 7.5 grams. That falls directly out of the cup standard: a cup is 120 g and holds 16 tablespoons, so each one is 7.5 g, and each teaspoon is a third of that at 2.5 g.

The number is straightforward. What is not straightforward is the measurement, because at this size the cup stops being useful and the spoon becomes the tool — and a spoon is as much a heap as a volume.

Where the Cup Method Stops Working

A measuring cup has a fixed rim, and levelling across it produces a repeatable volume. A tablespoon has a rim too, but it is small enough that the shape of the heap above it is a large fraction of the total. That is the whole problem.

  • Level tablespoon, spooned and swept — about 7.5 g of all-purpose flour
  • Level tablespoon, packed from the bag — around 9 g, because the flour compresses against the spoon
  • Heaped tablespoon — 12 g or more, depending on how much the flour mounds
  • Scant or rounded tablespoon — anywhere from 6 to 10 g, depending entirely on the cook

Between a scant spoon and a heaped one there is a 100% swing, on a quantity that recipes use for seasoning-level adjustments. Nobody resolves that with technique; they resolve it by weighing.

Seven and a Half Grams Is Below the Useful Resolution of a Spoon

The general rule this site applies throughout is that volume measurement for flour is reliable to about 20%. At a full cup that is 25 g, which is at least a meaningful quantity of flour. At a tablespoon it is 1.5 g, and a 1.5 g error in a recipe that uses two tablespoons of flour is not the same problem — it is proportionally the same, and 20% of a small number is still small.

The difficulty is that the error does not stay at 20%. It grows, because the heap is a bigger fraction of the spoon than it is of the cup. Roughly, a spoonful of flour is reliable to perhaps 30%, and that is the point at which the tool stops being an instrument and becomes an approximation.

Where a Tablespoon of Flour Shows Up

  • Thickening — a small amount of flour or a slurry stirred into a sauce at the end, where volume is not critical because the cook can adjust.
  • Dusting — flour for a greased tin, or for flouring a small work surface.
  • Adjusting a dough — a spoonful or two added when a mixture is too wet, where the judgment is tactile rather than numerical.
  • Small coatings — flouring pieces of fish or fruit before frying, where coverage matters more than weight.

Notice that none of those uses is precision-critical. That is not a coincidence: a tablespoon of flour appears in recipes where the exact quantity is less important than the fact that some flour is present. When a recipe does need an exact small quantity, it will normally give the figure in grams.

When a Spoonful Is Fine and When It Is Not

There is a case for calm here. Most recipes calling for a tablespoon of flour are using it as a thickener or a coating, and a gram either way changes the result by an amount no one could detect. Measuring flour to the gram in those situations adds effort and removes no risk.

The exception is baking, where a small flour quantity is part of a formula. In a two-tablespoon addition to a batter, the flour is around 5% of the dry weight, and a 30% error in the spoon becomes a 1.5% error in the whole mix. That is survivable. It becomes a problem only when several small quantities are each measured loosely, and the errors accumulate in the same direction. Which is the argument for weighing the small things rather than the large ones — the large ones are the forgiving ones.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 tablespoon of flour?

A1: 7.5 grams of all-purpose flour, level. That follows from the cup standard, since a cup is 120 grams and holds 16 tablespoons.

Q2: How many teaspoons is a tablespoon of flour?

A2: Three. A tablespoon is three teaspoons, so a tablespoon of flour is 7.5 grams and a teaspoon is 2.5 grams.

Q3: Why is a tablespoon of flour so hard to measure?

A3: Because a spoonful of flour can be heaped or level, and the difference between those two states is larger than the total quantity. A heaped tablespoon of flour can weigh over 12 grams, nearly double the level measure — at this size the technique matters more than the number.
