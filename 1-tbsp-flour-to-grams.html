How Many Grams in 1 Tablespoon of Flour?

1 US tablespoon of all-purpose flour equals 7.5 grams when measured using the spoon-and-level method. This is based on the standard density of 120g per US cup ÷ 16 tablespoons. Note that how you fill the tablespoon matters — a heaping tablespoon can weigh 10g or more.

The Conversion Formula (Tbsp → Grams)

  • Grams = Tablespoons × (Grams-per-Cup ÷ 16)
  • All-Purpose Flour: 1 tbsp × (120 ÷ 16) = 7.5 grams
  • Bread Flour: 1 tbsp × (130 ÷ 16) = 8.1 grams
  • Cake Flour: 1 tbsp × (110 ÷ 16) = 6.9 grams

When Tbsp Accuracy Matters

Tablespoon measurements for flour appear most often in small-scale recipes — roux for a single serving of gravy, thickening a pan sauce, or dusting a work surface. The 7.5g-per-tablespoon baseline is reliable for these small-scale uses, but for any quantity above 4 tablespoons (1/4 cup), switching to a scale gives you better precision. Flour compresses: a firmly packed tablespoon can weigh 10g or more — a 33% error.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 tablespoon of all-purpose flour?

A1: One US tablespoon of all-purpose flour weighs approximately 7.5 grams, based on 120g per US cup ÷ 16 tablespoons. Use a level tablespoon — not heaping — for this conversion to be accurate.

Q2: How many tablespoons of flour equal 30 grams?

A2: 30 grams of all-purpose flour equals 4 tablespoons (which is exactly 1/4 cup). For bread flour, 30g equals about 3.7 tablespoons; for cake flour, about 4.3 tablespoons.

Q3: Is measuring flour by tablespoon reliable?

A3: For 1–3 tablespoons — yes, it's usually fine and the error margin is small enough that most recipes won't break. For 4+ tablespoons, switch to a scale or use our grams-to-cups converters for better accuracy.
