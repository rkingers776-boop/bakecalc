How to Accurately Convert 50 Grams of Sugar to Cups

In cooking and baking, converting grams (a unit of mass/weight) into cups (a unit of physical volume) requires checking the specific ingredient's physical density. 50 grams of heavy granulated white sugar takes up significantly less space inside a measuring cup than 50 grams of fluffy powdered sugar. For the best culinary results, always align your kitchen conversions with the correct sugar subcategory.

The Mathematical Conversion Formula

The culinary conversion equation translates mass to volume using known base substance metrics:

  • Total Cup Volume = Given Weight in Grams ÷ Standard Ingredient Weight Per Cup
  • Granulated: 50g ÷ 200g/cup = 0.25 cups (1/4 cup)
  • Powdered: 50g ÷ 120g/cup = 0.42 cups
  • Packed Brown: 50g ÷ 220g/cup = 0.23 cups

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 50g of regular granulated white sugar?

A1: 50 grams of standard granulated white sugar is equal to exactly 0.25 cups, which perfectly translates to an accurate 1/4 cup measurement.

Q2: Does 50g of brown sugar equal the same cup volume as white sugar?

A2: No. Brown sugar contains moisture and is traditionally packed down into measuring cups, making it denser. 1 cup of packed brown sugar weighs roughly 220 grams, meaning 50 grams equals about 0.23 cups (slightly less than 1/4 cup).

Q3: What is the most precise way to measure 50g of sugar without a digital scale?

A3: If a kitchen scale is unavailable, measuring out exactly 4 flat, level tablespoons of granulated sugar will match the 50-gram metric precisely, as 1 tablespoon of granulated sugar holds roughly 12.5 grams.
