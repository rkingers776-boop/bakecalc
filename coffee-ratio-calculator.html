My Year of Bad Coffee (And the One Thing That Fixed It)

For about a year, I made coffee every morning with a pour-over dripper and consistently produced cups that were either watery and sour or bitter and undrinkable. I blamed the beans. I blamed the grinder. I blamed the water temperature. I bought a gooseneck kettle — which helped, but didn't fix it. I bought a burr grinder — which helped a lot, but still didn't fix it.

The problem was the ratio. I was using "two heaping tablespoons" of beans per mug — which, it turns out, was anywhere from 10 to 16 grams depending on the bean density, the grind size, and how generous my "heaping" was that morning. A 10g:300mL brew is a 1:30 ratio — weak, thin, sour. A 16g:300mL is roughly 1:19 — decent but still on the weak side for my taste.

I bought a $15 coffee scale with 0.1g precision. The next morning, I weighed 20g of beans (1:15 ratio for 300mL water). The coffee was perfect. Not "better" — perfect. Same beans, same grinder, same kettle, same dripper. The only variable that changed was the ratio being exact instead of estimated. I have not eyeballed coffee since.

Grind Size: The Other Dial

Ratio controls strength (how much coffee is dissolved in the water). Grind size controls extraction (what got dissolved from the grounds). They're separate variables but they interact:

  • Too fine → over-extracted → bitter, hollow, astringent. The water pulled out too much, including bitter tannins you don't want. Grind coarser.
  • Too coarse → under-extracted → sour, salty, thin. The water didn't pull enough out. Grind finer.

Troubleshooting rule: if your coffee tastes bad, change ONE variable at a time. Decide if it's a strength problem (ratio) or an extraction problem (grind). Sour = under-extracted = grind finer. Bitter = over-extracted = grind coarser. Weak = ratio too high (too much water) = use more coffee. This simple diagnostic framework will fix 90% of home brewing problems.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the best coffee-to-water ratio?

1:16 (18.8g coffee per 300mL water) is the consensus starting point for pour over and drip. Adjust based on taste — 1:15 for stronger, 1:17 for milder. French press: 1:15. Cold brew: 1:5 (concentrate). Use the calculator above for your specific method and water amount.

Why weigh coffee instead of using tablespoons?

A tablespoon of coffee can be 5-7g depending on grind size, bean density, and roast level. A 300mL brew at 1:16 needs 18.8g — that's 2.7 to 3.8 "tablespoons," a range too wide for consistency. A $15 0.1g scale eliminates this variable entirely.

Does grind size change the ratio?

No. Ratio controls strength. Grind size controls extraction. Keep your ratio constant and adjust grind to fix taste: sour → finer grind, bitter → coarser grind.
