The Problem With Cooking Converters (And How This One Is Different)

Most cooking converters do exactly one thing. Ounces to grams. Or cups to mL. Or tablespoons to milliliters. If your recipe uses three different measurement systems — which a shocking number do — you're opening three different converter tabs while your butter browns too fast and your timer goes off.

This page puts the six conversions you'll actually use on one screen:

  1. Fluid oz → mL — for when a British recipe says "8 fl oz milk" and your measuring jug is in mL.
  2. tbsp → mL — for when an Australian recipe's tablespoon (20 mL) doesn't match your American measuring spoon (14.79 mL).
  3. Cups → mL — for when a Japanese rice cooker cup (200 mL) is different from your US measuring cup (236.6 mL).
  4. Grams → oz — for when a European recipe says 100g chocolate and your brain thinks in ounces.
  5. Pounds → kg — for bulk buying, meat, and produce at metric-using stores.
  6. Cups → grams (ingredient-specific) — because 1 cup of honey in grams is not the same as 1 cup of flour in grams.

Scroll to the section you need, type your number, and the answer is there. No sign-up, no paywall, no app download. It works on your phone at the grocery store and on your laptop with floury hands.

When To Weigh Instead of Converting

This tool is fast, but it has limits. For dry ingredients — especially flour, cocoa powder, and powdered sugar — volume-to-weight conversions are always estimates. A "cup of flour" can weigh anywhere from 110g to 150g depending on how it got into the cup. The converter uses the standard spoon-and-level density (120g/cup), which is what recipe developers assume you're doing. If you scoop flour straight from the bag, your actual weight will be 15-25% higher than the converter says.

If you bake more than once a week: buy a digital kitchen scale. They cost $12. Put your mixing bowl on it, tare between ingredients, and add everything by weight. You'll get consistent results every single time, and you'll wash half as many measuring cups. This is the single piece of baking advice I give everyone, and the people who take it never go back.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which cooking conversions does this tool handle?

Fluid oz to mL, tablespoons to mL (US/UK/Australian), cups to mL (US/metric/Japanese), grams to ounces (weight), pounds to kilograms, and cups to grams for 20 common baking ingredients. Six converters, one scrollable page.

Why not just use one universal formula for mL→grams?

Because mL→grams depends on the ingredient's density. Water is 1 g/mL. Oil is 0.92 g/mL. Honey is 1.42 g/mL. Flour is 0.53 g/mL. A single formula would be wrong 90% of the time. The cups→grams section above uses the correct density for each ingredient individually.
