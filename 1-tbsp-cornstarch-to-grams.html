1 Tablespoon Cornstarch = 8 Grams. The Only Thickening Ratio You Need to Memorize.

8 grams of cornstarch thickens 1 cup (237ml) of liquid to a light gravy consistency — pourable, coating the back of a spoon, but not stiff. Write that on a Post-it and stick it inside your spice cabinet. It's the single most useful ratio in sauce-making, and once you know the gram amount (8g per cup of liquid), you can scale up or down forever without consulting another recipe.

For a medium-thick sauce that holds its shape on a plate: 12g per cup (1.5 tbsp). For a pie filling that slices cleanly: 16g per cup (2 tbsp). For an Asian stir-fry sauce that just barely clings to the vegetables: 4g per cup (½ tbsp). The ratio is linear — double the liquid, double the cornstarch. There is no mystery here, just math.

Cornstarch Slurry Basics (in Grams)

Cornstarch must be dissolved in cold liquid before it goes anywhere near heat. Hot liquid + dry cornstarch = lumps that will never dissolve. The fix is a slurry: mix the cornstarch with an equal amount of cold water (8g cornstarch + 8g cold water, roughly 1.5 teaspoons of water), stir until smooth, then whisk into your hot liquid. It'll thicken within 30–60 seconds of reaching a simmer. If it hasn't thickened after a full minute at a simmer, you didn't use enough starch. If it's gloppy and gelatinous, you used too much. Unlike flour-based roux, cornstarch-thickened sauces don't need long cooking to lose a raw flavor — once they've simmered for a minute, they're done.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 tablespoon of cornstarch?

1 US tablespoon of cornstarch weighs 8.0 grams (128g/cup ÷ 16). 1 teaspoon = approximately 2.7 grams.

Q2: Cornstarch vs flour for thickening — which is stronger?

Cornstarch has roughly twice the thickening power of flour by weight. 8g of cornstarch thickens 1 cup of liquid; you'd need about 15g of flour (2 tbsp) for the same result. Cornstarch also produces a clearer, glossier sauce — flour-based sauces are opaque and matte. For a fruit pie, cornstarch is almost always the better choice because it lets the fruit color show through. For a gravy, flour (or a mix of both) because the opacity looks right.
