8 oz to mL Converter

Convert 8 US fluid ounces to millilitres — one cup exactly, and the ounce figure that causes the most expensive mistakes.

*Based on standard US Customary fluid ounce calculations (1 fl oz ≈ 29.57353 mL).

8 oz in mL — the short answer

Eight US fluid ounces is 236.59 mL — 8 × 29.5735 = 236.5884. Under the imperial fluid ounce it is 227.30 mL. Eight ounces is the most useful figure in the whole ounce range, and also the most dangerous, and both facts come from the same source: eight is the number where the fluid ounce and the cup are defined to be equal.

One Cup, Exactly

A US cup is 8 fluid ounces. That is not a conversion, it is the definition, and it makes 8 oz the only fluid ounce figure you can use without doing any arithmetic at all. A recipe asking for 8 oz of liquid and a recipe asking for 1 cup are asking for the same 236.59 mL.

It is also half a pint, which matters on the next page along: 16 fl oz is a full US pint, so 8 oz sits exactly halfway between a cup and a pint.

Where the Eight-Ounce Figure Turns Dangerous

Because 8 fl oz and 1 cup are the same thing for liquids, people carry the equivalence across to solids — and there it collapses. Eight ounces of weight is always 227 grams, but 227 grams occupies wildly different volumes depending on what it is:

  • Cream cheese — a standard 8 oz brick weighs 227 g and measures close to 1 cup. Here the substitution works, because cream cheese is a solid block with a density near that of water.
  • Shredded cheese — 8 oz by weight is 227 g, but those shreds fill roughly 2 cups. Treat it as 1 cup and you have used half the cheese the recipe wanted.
  • Chocolate chips — an 8 oz bag is 227 g and fills about 1 and a third cups. Measure one cup instead and you are 25% short on chocolate.
  • Chopped chocolate — same story as chips, for the same reason.

The mechanism is air. A block of cream cheese has essentially none inside it; a pile of shreds or chips is mostly gaps. Same mass, far more space. This is why an 8 oz bag of chocolate chips and a 1 cup measure are not interchangeable, and why the error is expensive: the chocolate is usually the most costly ingredient in the bowl, and it is also carrying a share of the fat and sugar that the rest of the formula was balanced against.

The rule that resolves all of it: if the package states a weight, use a scale. If a recipe states a volume, use a cup. Never translate one into the other for a solid whose shape traps air.

Where 8 oz Shows Up

  • Coffee — 8 oz is what a coffee cup is nominally sized for, though most mugs actually hold 10 to 12 fl oz.
  • Yogurt and sour cream — 8 oz tubs, labelled by weight.
  • Cream cheese — the standard brick is 8 oz, 227 g.
  • Bottles — 8 fl oz is the most common single-serve liquid size on the shelf.

Eight Ounces by Weight Is 227 Grams

Eight ounces of weight is 226.80 g, which the food industry rounds to 227. That number is worth committing to memory because it reappears constantly in American baking: 227 g is half a pound, and it is the weight of one US stick of butter.

So the same 227 g shows up as a stick of butter measuring half a cup, and as a brick of cream cheese measuring a full cup. Identical weight, double the volume — the clearest possible proof that ounces of weight and cups of volume are only related through the ingredient.

The Metric Equivalent People Actually Use

Recipes written in metric treat 8 fl oz as 240 mL. That is 1.4% high, and it is what nutrition labels mean by a cup as well, so in practice 8 oz and 240 mL are the same quantity in a kitchen.

Carry that to a different liquid and the equivalence breaks in a way that matters. Eight fluid ounces of honey weighs 336 g; eight fluid ounces of vegetable oil weighs about 218 g. Same volume, 54% difference in weight — which is why the answer to whether 8 oz is a cup always has to be qualified by what is in the cup.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many mL is 8 oz?

A1: 236.59 mL for 8 US fluid ounces. British and Australian recipes use the imperial fluid ounce, where 8 oz is 227.30 mL — about 4% less. US nutrition labels round the same quantity to 240 mL.

Q2: Is 8 oz the same as 1 cup?

A2: Yes for liquids: a US cup is defined as 8 fluid ounces, so the two are the same by definition. For solids it depends on the ingredient, because 8 ounces of weight varies widely in volume — shredded cheese fills about 2 cups, chocolate chips about 1 and a third.

Q3: Is 8 oz of chocolate chips the same as 1 cup?

A3: No, and the gap is large. An 8 oz bag of chocolate chips is 227 grams by weight, but 227 grams of chips occupy roughly 1 and a third cups because the chips leave air between them. Measuring one cup instead leaves you about 25% short on chocolate.
