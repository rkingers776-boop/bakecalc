Tablespoons to mL — Because Not All Tablespoons Are Equal

Your tablespoon might be 15 mL. Or 20 mL. Or 14.79 mL. Depends which country made your measuring spoons. Here's the full story.

The Tablespoon Problem: Three Countries, Three Sizes

Most people assume a tablespoon is a tablespoon. It's not. Three different standards exist, and they're different enough to matter:

Country1 tbsp = mLWhy
United States14.79 mL1/2 US fluid ounce. Standard since 1896.
United Kingdom / Metric15 mLRounded from 14.79. Used in most metric cookbooks.
Australia20 mL4 teaspoons × 5 mL. Adopted with metrication in 1970s.

The difference between a US tablespoon (14.79 mL) and an Australian one (20 mL) is 35%. That's the difference between a properly seasoned dish and an overseasoned one. If you're baking from an Australian Women's Weekly cookbook with American measuring spoons, you're adding a third less of every tablespoon-measured ingredient than the recipe intends.

When This Actually Matters (And When It Doesn't)

Doesn't matter much: soup, stew, sauce, marinade. A 5 mL difference in soy sauce for a stir-fry that serves four is barely detectable. Use whatever spoon you have.

Matters a lot: baking powder, baking soda, vanilla extract, salt in bread dough, gelatin, food coloring, any recipe where the tablespoon-measured ingredient is chemically active. A 35% overdose of baking powder makes cakes rise and collapse. A 35% underdose of gelatin means your panna cotta never sets.

Matters critically: medicine. Never use a kitchen tablespoon for liquid medication. A US tablespoon (14.79 mL) vs an Australian tablespoon (20 mL) vs a random flatware spoon (8-18 mL) could mean under- or over-dosing. Use the dosing cup or syringe that comes with the medicine.

The Flatware Spoon Trap

Go to your silverware drawer right now and grab a tablespoon — the big spoon you eat soup with. Fill it with water and pour it into a measuring spoon. I'll wait.

It probably held somewhere between 8 and 18 mL, didn't it? That's because flatware spoons are designed by industrial designers trying to make them look good, not metrologists trying to make them accurate. A heavy, deep-bowled soup spoon might hold 18 mL. A slim, shallow "continental" style spoon might hold 8 mL. Neither one is a measuring tablespoon, and neither should be used as one.

Measuring spoons cost about $5 for a set. Flatware tablespoons are for eating, not measuring. This is one of those things where being cheap costs you more in ruined ingredients than the tool would have cost in the first place.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many mL is 1 US tablespoon?

One US customary tablespoon equals 14.79 milliliters (exactly 1/2 US fluid ounce). Most US recipes round to 15 mL in practice, but the legal definition is 14.7867648 mL.

Why is an Australian tablespoon 20 mL?

When Australia metricated in the 1970s, they defined 1 teaspoon = 5 mL and 1 tablespoon = 4 teaspoons = 20 mL. This is actually more logical than the US system (where 1 tbsp = 3 tsp = 14.79 mL, making a US teaspoon 4.93 mL). The metric system values clean base-10 relationships.

Can I use a regular spoon instead of a measuring tablespoon?

No. Flatware tablespoons vary from 8 to 18 mL. Measuring spoons are standardized. For baking and any recipe where precision matters, use proper measuring spoons. The $5 they cost is less than the ingredients you'll waste on a failed recipe.
