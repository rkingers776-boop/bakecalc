Understanding 200 Grams of Sugar in Recipe Scaling

At 200 grams, you are dealing with exactly 1 full level cup (1.00 cups precisely). Because sugar types vary dramatically in density — granulated is dense and crystalline, powdered is light and aerated, and brown sugar contains sticky molasses — knowing the exact conversion for your specific sweetener is essential for consistent results.

The Mathematical Conversion Formula

The calculation divides the sugar mass by the specific density constant for each variety:

  • Total Cup Volume = Given Weight in Grams ÷ Base Ingredient Unit per Cup
  • Granulated White: 200g ÷ 200g/cup = 1.00 cups (1 cup)
  • Packed Brown: 200g ÷ 220g/cup = 0.91 cups
  • Powdered: 200g ÷ 120g/cup = 1.67 cups

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 200g of granulated white sugar?

A1: 200 grams of granulated white sugar equals exactly 1 full US customary measuring cup.

Q2: How do I measure 200g of sugar with standard kitchen cups?

A2: A single level 1-cup measure of standard granulated sugar gives you the correct 200g weight.

Q3: What if my recipe uses a different sugar type for 200g?

A3: While granulated sugar measures cleanly, confectioners sugar at 200g would overflow to 1.67 cups — always verify which sugar type your recipe intends.
