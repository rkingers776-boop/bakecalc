6 oz to mL Converter

Convert 6 US fluid ounces to millilitres — three quarters of a cup, and the fraction most measuring sets forget.

Milliliter Equivalent:

177.44 mL

*Based on standard US Customary fluid ounce calculations (1 fl oz ≈ 29.57353 mL).

6 oz in mL — the short answer

Six US fluid ounces is 177.44 mL — 6 × 29.5735 = 177.4410. Under the imperial fluid ounce the same words mean 170.48 mL. Six ounces is one of the figures that divides neatly into a cup, and yet it still causes more trouble in a kitchen than it should, for a reason that has nothing to do with arithmetic.

Three Quarters of a Cup — the Measure Nobody Owns

Six fluid ounces is exactly 3/4 cup. That is a clean fraction, and it is also the fraction missing from almost every measuring cup set sold in the United States. The standard set is 1/4, 1/3, 1/2 and 1 cup. There is no 3/4 anywhere in the drawer.

So a recipe asking for 6 fl oz forces a decision:

  • A half cup measure plus a quarter cup measure — two scoops, no counting.
  • Twelve tablespoons, counted in two passes of six.
  • A metric jug, where 177 mL sits right there on the scale.

None of those is difficult, and that is the point: 6 oz is the clearest example of a quantity that becomes trivial the moment you have a scale or a millilitre jug, and requires a small plan without one.

Where 6 oz Shows Up

  • Coffee — the small size at several chains, and the traditional measure for a single serving of filter coffee in a diner.
  • Tomato paste — small cans are labelled 6 oz, which is a weight. See the section below, because the volume is not what you would expect.
  • Juice — a traditional juice glass pour is about 6 fl oz.
  • Cocktails — the middle volume in a number of large-format drinks.

Six Ounces by Weight Is 170 Grams

Six ounces of weight is 170.10 grams; six fluid ounces of volume is 177.44 mL. The two are 4% apart in raw numbers, which is close enough to be genuinely deceptive.

Tomato paste is the clean illustration. A 6 oz can holds 170 g by weight, but tomato paste is dense and is packed into the can with no air gaps, so 170 g occupies roughly 162 mL. That is 9% less than the 177.44 mL you would pour if you treated the can as a fluid measure. Cans are labelled by weight, full stop, and dense pastes are where the assumption costs the most.

Why the Cup Set Skips Three Quarters

The four-cup set covers more fractions with fewer objects than any other combination. A quarter, a third, a half and a whole can build three quarters (half plus quarter), two thirds (third plus third) and five sixths (half plus third). Adding a dedicated 3/4 cup measure would duplicate a quantity the set already reaches.

So the missing measure is a design decision rather than an oversight. It still trips people up during a conversion, which is a different problem from having the wrong number.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many mL is 6 oz?

A1: 177.44 mL for 6 US fluid ounces. Under the imperial fluid ounce used in Britain and Australia, 6 oz is 170.48 mL, roughly 4% less.

Q2: How many cups is 6 oz?

A2: Three quarters of a cup, which is 12 tablespoons. Most US measuring cup sets do not include a 3/4 cup measure, so build it from a half cup plus a quarter cup, or count out 12 tablespoons.

Q3: Is 6 oz of tomato paste the same as 6 fluid ounces?

A3: No. A 6 oz can of tomato paste is labelled by weight — 170 grams. Because tomato paste is dense and packed solidly, that occupies roughly 162 mL, about 9% less than the 177.44 mL that 6 fluid ounces would fill.
