32 oz in mL — the short answer

Thirty-two US fluid ounces is 946.35 mL — 32 × 29.5735 = 946.3529, which is one US quart. Under the imperial fluid ounce the same figure is 909.22 mL, and an imperial quart is a different thing again at 1136.52 mL. Thirty-two is the last clean stop in the American system before the gallon, and it is also the number where volume and weight both happen to land on something memorable.

One Quart

A US quart is 32 fluid ounces, which is 4 cups, which is 2 pints. It is a quarter of a gallon, and the gallon is 128 fl oz, so the whole ladder is powers of two: 8, 16, 32, 128. Nothing in that chain requires a non-integer conversion, which is precisely why the system survived in kitchens.

The practical equivalence to hold onto is four cups. A quart container holds four cups of liquid, and it also happens to be about the capacity you need for the batter of a 9 by 13 inch sheet cake — which is why quart containers are standard equipment in American kitchens and why the figure keeps coming up in recipe scaling.

The Same Number Is Also Two Pounds

Thirty-two ounces of weight is 907.18 g, which is 2 pounds. So 32 oz is one of the rare figures where both readings produce a whole unit: a quart by volume, two pounds by mass. That symmetry is a coincidence of a base-16 weight system meeting a base-8 volume system, and it is exactly what makes the number treacherous.

  • 32 fl oz of honey — 946 mL of volume, weighing about 1362 g, or 3 pounds.
  • 32 oz of honey by weight — 907 g, occupying about 630 mL, or 2.7 cups.

One ingredient, two readings, a 50% difference in how much honey ends up in the bowl. A recipe that says 32 oz of honey almost certainly means weight, because nobody measures honey by volume if they can avoid it — and yet the fluid-ounce reading is waiting there for anyone who assumes the number is a volume.

Where 32 oz Shows Up

  • Water bottles — 32 oz is the classic capacity for an insulated bottle and for the Nalgene shape, and this is the most common reason the conversion gets searched.
  • Yogurt tubs — large tubs are sold as 32 oz, by weight.
  • Stock and broth — boxed broth is often packed at 32 fl oz, matching a quart.
  • Beer — the 32 oz can, called a crowler, is a brewery fill size.

Where the Quart Sits in the American System

The quart is the last doubling before the gallon, and it is the unit most likely to trip up a cook working from metric. The reason is not the arithmetic but the feel of the number: a litre is 1000 mL and sits just above a quart at 946.35 mL, so the two are close enough to be casually interchanged and far enough apart that doing so accumulates error.

Substitute a litre for a quart three times in a large batch and you have added over 160 mL of extra liquid — about two thirds of a cup. That is the sort of difference that shows up as a soggy result rather than an obviously wrong one, which is worse.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many mL is 32 oz?

A1: 946.35 mL for 32 US fluid ounces, which is one US quart. Under the imperial fluid ounce the same figure is 909.22 mL — and an imperial quart is 1136.52 mL, so the two quarts differ by 20%.

Q2: How many cups is 32 oz?

A2: Four cups, for a liquid. A US cup is 8 fluid ounces, so 32 fluid ounces is 4 cups, 2 pints, or one quart. Thirty-two ounces of a solid by weight is 907 grams — 2 pounds — and its volume depends on the ingredient.

Q3: Is 32 oz the same as 2 pounds?

A3: By weight, yes: 32 ounces is 2 pounds, or 907 grams. By volume, no: 32 fluid ounces is 946 mL and has nothing to do with pounds. The coincidence that both readings land on neat figures is why this number causes so much confusion.
