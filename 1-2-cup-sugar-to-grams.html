How Many Grams in 1/2 Cup of Sugar?

Half a cup of sugar is one of the most common measurements in cookie, muffin, and quick-bread recipes — and ½ cup of granulated white sugar equals exactly 100 grams. Brown sugar (packed) comes in at ~110 grams per half cup, while powdered sugar is a much lighter ~60 grams. These differences are large enough to matter, especially in recipes where sugar does more than sweeten — it also affects spread, browning, and texture.

The Conversion Formula

Half the full-cup density for each sugar variety:

  • Grams = Cups × Grams-per-Cup
  • Granulated Sugar: 0.5 × 200 g = 100 grams
  • Brown Sugar (packed): 0.5 × 220 g = 110 grams
  • Powdered Sugar: 0.5 × 120 g = 60 grams

Why Powdered Sugar Is So Much Lighter

Powdered sugar (also called confectioners' or icing sugar) is granulated sugar ground to a fine dust and mixed with a small amount of cornstarch to prevent caking. The fine grind incorporates a tremendous amount of air — which is why 1 cup weighs only 120 g versus granulated sugar's 200 g. When a frosting recipe calls for "1/2 cup powdered sugar, sifted," sifting adds even more air, and the weight can drop below 55 grams. For consistent results, weigh powdered sugar whenever possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1/2 cup of granulated sugar?

A1: One half cup of granulated white sugar weighs exactly 100 grams — half of the standard 200 g full-cup baseline. It's one of the easiest conversions in baking.

Q2: How much does 1/2 cup of packed brown sugar weigh?

A2: When firmly packed, ½ cup of brown sugar weighs roughly 110 grams. The extra 10 g versus white sugar comes from molasses content and the dense packing method.

Q3: Is 1/2 cup of powdered sugar the same weight as granulated?

A3: No — ½ cup of powdered sugar weighs only ~60 g, while granulated is 100 g per half cup. Substituting by volume without adjusting for this 40 g difference will dramatically change your recipe.
