1/2 cup of sugar in grams — the short answer

Half a cup of granulated sugar is 100 grams — exactly. A cup weighs 200 g, and the half cup divides it evenly, which makes this one of the cleanest conversions on the site.

Clean numbers are rare in baking conversion, and when they turn up they usually indicate that the measurement itself is reliable. That is the case here, and the reason is worth understanding because it separates sugar from flour in an important way.

Why Sugar Does Not Need a Scale

Flour is compressible. Its particles are soft and irregular, so scooping presses them together and the same cup can hold 120 g or 145 g depending on technique. Granulated sugar is crystalline: hard, uniform grains that rest on each other and cannot be packed tighter by anything a measuring cup can do.

So the volume method behaves differently for the two ingredients:

  • A cup of sugar, spooned — about 200 g
  • A cup of sugar, scooped from the bag — about 201 g
  • A cup of flour, spooned — 120 g
  • A cup of flour, scooped from the bag — 145 g or more

Half a cup of sugar is therefore 100 g whether it was scooped or spooned, and the 0.5% variation is smaller than the tolerance of most recipes. That is a genuinely useful fact: it means sugar can be scaled up and down by volume without the systematic drift that flour suffers.

The Exception Is Brown Sugar

Brown sugar is granulated sugar with molasses in it, and the molasses makes it damp and compressible. That breaks the rule. The same half cup can hold anywhere from 90 to 115 grams depending on how firmly it was pressed in, which is why recipes specify packed, and why packed is a real instruction rather than a figure of speech.

Packed means pressed into the measure with the back of a spoon until it holds the shape of the cup when turned out. Done properly, a cup of brown sugar is about 220 g. Done loosely, it is closer to 180 g. On half a cup that is a 20-gram spread, which is the same order of error flour suffers from.

Half a Cup of Every Sugar

  • Granulated white sugar — 100 g
  • Brown sugar, packed — 110 g
  • Brown sugar, loosely spooned — 90 g
  • Powdered sugar, unsifted — 60 g
  • Powdered sugar, sifted — 50 g
  • Coconut sugar — 90 g
  • Turbinado or raw sugar — 100 g

Powdered sugar is the outlier in the other direction. Its particles are so fine and carry so much air that half a cup weighs little more than half as much as granulated. Substituting it by volume for granulated sugar in a cake would under-sweeten the batter while adding cornstarch, which is a different problem again.

Where Half a Cup of Sugar Shows Up

Half a cup is the sugar quantity in a single-layer cake, a batch of muffins, a buttercream for a small cake, and most glazes and simple syrups. In a simple syrup the half cup is paired with half a cup of water by volume — which is 100 g of sugar and 118 g of water, an unequal pairing by weight that nevertheless produces the standard 1 to 1 syrup because it is a volume ratio, not a mass one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1/2 cup of sugar?

A1: Exactly 100 grams of granulated sugar. A cup weighs 200 grams, so half is 100 — one of the roundest conversions in the kitchen.

Q2: Does sugar measure as precisely as flour by cup?

A2: More precisely. Sugar crystals are hard and uniform and do not compress under a measuring cup, so a scooped cup and a spooned cup land within a couple of grams of each other. Flour can differ by 25 grams between the same two methods.

Q3: Is 1/2 cup of brown sugar also 100 g?

A3: No. Brown sugar packed into a cup weighs about 220 grams, so half a cup is roughly 110 grams. It is also worth being deliberate about packing — loose brown sugar in the same cup weighs about 180 grams, so unpacked it would be only 90.
