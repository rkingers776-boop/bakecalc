1 Cup Vegetable Oil = 218 Grams. Here's Why That Number Isn't 237.

Water weighs 237 grams per US cup. Oil weighs 218 grams. The 19-gram gap exists because oil is less dense than water — about 0.92 grams per milliliter vs water's 1.00. That's also why oil floats on water and why vinaigrettes separate. If you've ever poured oil into a measuring cup and noticed it looks like more than the line suggests, you're not crazy — it's just physics. The same volume takes up the same space, but the weight is different. Your measuring cup measures volume, not mass, and that's the entire reason tools like this exist.

Most American recipes that call for "vegetable oil" mean the neutral, pale-yellow stuff from a plastic jug — usually soybean or canola oil. These clock in at 218g per cup. Sunflower oil is functionally identical at 218g. Olive oil is the slight outlier at 216g — a 2-gram difference you won't notice in a single batch of muffins but might care about if you're costing recipes down to the penny.

The Oil-for-Butter Swap That Actually Works

Butter is 227g per cup. Vegetable oil is 218g. The 9-gram difference is small, but butter has something oil doesn't: water. About 15-17% of butter is water, and that water turns to steam in the oven, creating lift and tenderness. Straight oil — 100% fat, zero water — behaves differently. If you swap 1 cup butter for 1 cup oil in a cake recipe, you'll get a moister crumb but less rise and a slightly greasy mouthfeel. The fix isn't complicated: reduce the oil to ¾ cup (about 164g) and add 2-3 tablespoons of whatever liquid the recipe already uses (milk, water, buttermilk) to make up for the missing water content. This gets you closer to the original texture than a straight 1:1 swap ever will.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 cup of vegetable oil?

One US cup of vegetable oil (canola, corn, soybean, or generic blend) weighs 218 grams. This is based on oil density of approximately 0.92 g/ml. Water at 237g/cup is about 8.7% heavier.

Q2: Does the brand of vegetable oil change the weight?

Not meaningfully. All refined vegetable oils from major producers — Crisco, Wesson, store brands — fall within 1-2 grams of 218g per cup. The refining process standardizes density. Cold-pressed or artisanal oils can vary slightly more, but still within a 3-gram window.

Q3: What if my recipe just says "oil" — which conversion do I use?

Assume 218g per cup. In American recipes, unspecified "oil" defaults to vegetable oil or canola oil. If the recipe writer intended olive oil, they almost always specify it — partly for flavor reasons, partly because olive oil costs more and people want to know when they're using it.
