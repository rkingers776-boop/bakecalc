5 oz to mL Converter

Convert 5 US fluid ounces to millilitres — the restaurant wine pour, five eighths of a cup, and the canned-goods trap.

Milliliter Equivalent:

147.87 mL

*Based on standard US Customary fluid ounce calculations (1 fl oz ≈ 29.57353 mL).

5 oz in mL — the short answer

Five US fluid ounces is 147.87 mL — 5 × 29.5735 = 147.8675. Under the imperial fluid ounce it is 142.07 mL. Unlike most figures on this site, 5 oz has a well-known life outside baking, and that is what drives most of the searches for it.

Five Ounces Is the Standard Glass of Wine

Five fluid ounces is the standard restaurant pour for a glass of wine in the United States. That single fact is why this conversion gets looked up far more often than its neighbours: people are trying to work out how much they are actually drinking, or how many glasses a bottle really contains.

  • A 750 mL bottle divided into 147.87 mL pours gives almost exactly five glasses, with about 11 mL left over as spillage and the last partial pour.
  • In the glass, 5 fl oz sits a little below the widest point of a medium wine glass — less than most people estimate by eye.
  • Standard pours shrink with the wine. Dessert wines are served at 2 to 3 oz because both sugar and alcohol are higher, and fortified wines such as port and sherry are similar.

Five ounces is the benchmark for dry table wine. Everything else is a deliberate reduction from it.

In the Kitchen, 5 oz Is Five Eighths of a Cup

Five fluid ounces is 5/8 cup, or 10 tablespoons. Since five eighths is not a fraction any recipe writer reaches for, formulas that call for 5 oz usually give millilitres or a weight alongside. If you do need to measure it, 10 tablespoons is the reliable route — count twice.

This is also why 5 oz is much rarer in baking than 4 oz. Four ounces is exactly half a cup; five ounces is nothing in particular. The number shows up in baking mostly as a weight in small cans and portion labels rather than as a volume in a formula.

Where 5 oz Shows Up Besides the Wine Glass

  • Baby bottles — the step between the 4 oz and 6 oz marks.
  • Single-serve desserts — larger yogurt and pudding cups.
  • Small cans — tuna and chicken are sold in 5 oz cans, but that figure is a weight. See below.
  • Nutrition labels — 5 oz is a common reference portion for meat and fish.

The Canned-Goods Trap

Ounces on a can are always a weight. A 5 oz can of tuna holds 142 grams of fish, whatever the drained volume turns out to be. A 5 oz can of evaporated milk is the same story: the label describes mass.

Evaporated milk is where the two readings visibly diverge. By weight, 5 oz is 142 g. By volume, 5 fl oz is 147.87 mL. Evaporated milk has a density near 1.06 g/mL, so 5 fl oz of it would weigh about 157 g — meaning the 5 oz on the can and the 5 fl oz you might pour are off by roughly 10% from each other. Both labels are accurate; they are answering different questions.

The rule holds across every aisle: no fl, no volume.

Why Wine Is Measured in Ounces at All

American alcohol regulation and portion standards have always been written in fluid ounces, so the hospitality industry follows. A 5 oz glass of 12% wine contains about 0.6 fl oz of pure alcohol, and that is where the definition of a standard drink comes from — the figure exists for regulation and public health messaging more than for cooking.

If you measure wine in millilitres instead, the standard pour is 147.87 mL, and most people quietly use 150. That is a 1.4% difference, which is irrelevant at restaurant speed and worth avoiding if you are tracking intake or building a cocktail to a ratio. Use 147 or 148 and the arithmetic stays honest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many mL is 5 oz?

A1: 147.87 mL for 5 US fluid ounces. Under the imperial fluid ounce used in Britain and Australia, 5 oz is 142.07 mL — about 4% less.

Q2: How many cups is 5 oz?

A2: Five eighths of a cup, which is 10 tablespoons. That is an awkward quantity to measure with standard cups, so recipes using 5 fluid ounces usually give the figure in millilitres or by weight instead.

Q3: Is a 5 oz can of tuna 5 fluid ounces?

A3: No. Canned goods are labelled by weight, so a 5 oz can holds 142 grams of fish. The same applies to a 5 oz can of evaporated milk. Fluid ounces only apply when the label says fl oz.
