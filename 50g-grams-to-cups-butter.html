Understanding 50 Grams of Butter in Baking Metrics

When cooking sophisticated European or American pastries, converting 50 grams of butter to cups requires a clear understanding of fat density. Unlike flour, butter is incredibly dense. A standard US customary cup of solid stick butter weighs precisely 227 grams. Therefore, 50g represents a relatively small volumetric fraction, often corresponding to just under half of a standard stick of butter.

The Mathematical Conversion Formula

The calculation tracks fat mass divided by the specific culinary substance constants:

  • Total Cup Volume = Weight in Grams ÷ Butter Component Per Cup
  • Stick Butter: 50g ÷ 227g/cup = 0.22 cups
  • Melted Butter: 50g ÷ 240g/cup = 0.21 cups
  • Whipped Butter: 50g ÷ 150g/cup = 0.33 cups

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 50g of standard stick butter?

A1: 50 grams of stick butter is equal to approximately 0.22 cups, which functionally translates to slightly less than a level 1/4 measuring cup (or roughly 3.5 tablespoons).

Q2: How many tablespoons are in 50 grams of butter?

A2: One tablespoon of butter weighs roughly 14.2 grams. Therefore, 50 grams of butter yields exactly 3.5 tablespoons of solid fat block.

Q3: Why does whipped butter give a different cup value than solid butter?

A3: Whipped butter has tiny air bubbles incorporated into it during processing. This aeration lowers its overall density, meaning 50g of whipped butter fills more physical space in a cup (0.33 cups) than solid butter.
