How Many Grams in 1 Cup of Flour?

If you've ever encountered an American recipe calling for "1 cup of flour" while your kitchen scale only reads grams, you're not alone. The short answer: 1 US customary cup of all-purpose flour equals 120 grams. This is the gold-standard conversion used by King Arthur Baking, America's Test Kitchen, and most professional recipe developers. But the full story depends on which flour you're scooping and how you measure it.

The Conversion Formula (Cups → Grams)

The math is straightforward — multiply the number of cups by the grams-per-cup density of your specific flour:

  • Grams = Cups × Grams-per-Cup
  • All-Purpose Flour: 1 cup × 120 g/cup = 120 grams
  • Bread Flour: 1 cup × 130 g/cup = 130 grams
  • Cake Flour: 1 cup × 110 g/cup = 110 grams

Why the Spoon-and-Level Method Matters

Flour compacts easily. If you scoop directly from the bag with your measuring cup, you can pack up to 150 grams into a single cup — a 25% over-measure that yields dense, dry baked goods. The spoon-and-level method (spoon flour lightly into the cup, then level with a straight edge) consistently yields ~120 grams per cup and is what this converter assumes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 cup of all-purpose flour?

A1: One US customary cup of all-purpose flour weighs approximately 120 grams when measured using the spoon-and-level method. This is the standard baseline used by most American baking resources.

Q2: Does the type of flour change the 1-cup-to-grams conversion?

A2: Yes. One cup of bread flour weighs roughly 130 grams (higher protein = denser), while one cup of cake flour weighs about 110 grams (finer mill = lighter). Always identify which flour your recipe calls for before converting.

Q3: Why do professional bakers prefer grams over cups for flour?

A3: Flour is highly compressible — a packed cup can weigh up to 150 g versus 120 g spooned, a 25% error. Grams eliminate this variability completely, giving you the same result every single bake.
