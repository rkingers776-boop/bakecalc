Convert 12 US fluid ounces of water to millilitres — where volume and weight give the same number, and where that stops being true.

Milliliter Equivalent:

354.88 mL

*Calculated precisely using US customary liquid measures.

12 oz of water in mL — the short answer

Twelve US fluid ounces of water is 354.88 mL — 12 × 29.5735 = 354.8820. It also weighs 354.88 grams, and that is not a coincidence but the whole reason this page is short: water is the substance the metric system was calibrated against, so a millilitre of water is a gram of water by definition.

Under the imperial fluid ounce the same measure is 340.96 mL. For water specifically the distinction is worth caring about, because every other figure on the page — grams, cups, hydration percentages — is derived from the millilitres.

The Number That Matches Itself

For every other liquid, volume and weight are different numbers. For water they are the same, which makes water the fixed point that everything else in baking is measured against. A bread recipe's hydration of 75% means 75 grams of water for every 100 grams of flour — and because water's density is 1, that is also 75 mL. It is the only ingredient where a scale and a jug give the same answer.

That equivalence holds closely enough across the temperatures you cook at that it is safe to use. Water reaches its maximum density near 4 °C and thins slightly as it warms — at 100 °C it is about 4% lighter per millilitre — but nothing in a kitchen is precise enough for that to matter.

Why 12 oz Is the Most Familiar Water Volume

Twelve fluid ounces is the size of a standard drinks can, and it is also the nominal size of a coffee mug. That is why this particular conversion gets looked up more than its neighbours: people are not converting a recipe, they are working out what is in the container in their hand.

  • A soda can — 12 fl oz, or 354.88 mL, which is why cans sold in metric countries are labelled 355 mL.
  • A coffee mug — nominally 12 oz, though most mugs hold between 10 and 14 fl oz when filled to a usable level.
  • A beer bottle — the standard US bottle is 12 fl oz, the same size as the can.
  • A glass of water — eight glasses a day, in the advice everyone half-remembers, means 8 × 12 fl oz in some tellings and 8 × 8 fl oz in others. They are not the same advice.

Where the Equivalence Breaks Down

Water is a safe short-cut precisely because nothing else behaves like it. Twelve fluid ounces of any other liquid gives a weight that is not 354.88 g, and the further the ingredient is from water, the further the number drifts:

  • Milk — about 365 g, roughly 3% heavier than water.
  • Heavy cream — about 357 g, very close to water.
  • Vegetable oil — about 325 g, around 8% lighter than water.
  • Honey — about 504 g, more than 40% heavier than water.

So the habit of reading "12 oz" as "354 mL" is fine for water and progressively wrong for everything else. The heavier the sugar content and the lower the fat, the more the answer moves.

Twelve Ounces of Water in Baking

Water does more in a bake than hydrate flour. It is the solvent that lets yeast and enzymes work, the medium that carries heat through a custard, and the thing that turns to steam and creates lift in a laminated dough. In all three roles the quantity is set by weight, not volume — which is why a bread formula is written in percentages rather than cups.

When a recipe gives water in fluid ounces, the conversion is the easy part. What matters more is which other liquid the water is standing in for. Replacing water with milk gram for gram adds fat and protein and changes both browning and crumb; replacing it with buttermilk adds acid, which reacts with baking soda. Same 354.88 mL, different bread.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Exactly how many milliliters are in 12 oz of water?

A1: 354.88 mL for 12 US fluid ounces. Water is the one liquid where the arithmetic is trivial: 12 fluid ounces of water also weighs 354.88 grams, because water density is the reference point the metric system was built on.

Q2: Does 12 oz of water weigh the same as 12 oz of flour?

A2: No, and volume is the wrong tool for flour. Twelve fluid ounces of water weighs 354.88 g. Twelve fluid ounces of all-purpose flour is roughly 180 g, because flour is a compressible solid full of air pockets — it is measured by weight, not by fluid ounces.

Q3: How many cups is 12 oz of water?

A3: One and a half cups. A US cup is 8 fluid ounces, so 12 fluid ounces is 1.5 cups, which is also three quarters of a pint.
