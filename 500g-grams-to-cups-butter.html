Scaling 500 Grams of Butter for Bulk Recipe Batches

Working with 500 grams of butter means you are likely scaling a rich pastry dough, brioche, or large cake batch — roughly 2 cups plus 1.5 tablespoons (2.20 cups). Since standard US butter sticks cap at 113.4g each, and a cup of solid butter maxes out at 227g, larger butter weights require multi-stick and multi-cup calculations for accuracy.

The Mathematical Conversion Formula

The calculation divides butter mass by the density constant based on physical state:

  • Total Cup Volume = Weight in Grams ÷ Butter Component Per Cup
  • Stick Butter: 500g ÷ 227g/cup = 2.20 cups
  • Melted Butter: 500g ÷ 240g/cup = 2.08 cups
  • Whipped Butter: 500g ÷ 150g/cup = 3.33 cups

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 500g of standard solid butter?

A1: 500 grams of standard stick butter equals approximately 2.20 cups — just over 2 full cups.

Q2: How many sticks of butter do I need for 500g?

A2: 500g of butter equals about 4.4 US sticks. Use 2 full level cups plus about 1.5 extra tablespoons of solid butter.

Q3: Does melted butter measure differently than solid butter at 500g?

A3: Yes. Solid butter at 500g is 2.20 cups, but melted butter at 500g occupies only about 2.08 cups (240g/cup). The 0.12-cup difference matters at this scale — use solid measurements for accuracy unless melted is specified.
