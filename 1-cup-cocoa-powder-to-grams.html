How Many Grams in 1 Cup of Cocoa Powder?

If you're staring at a recipe that calls for "1 cup cocoa powder" and your scale only does grams, the short answer is 100 grams — assuming natural unsweetened cocoa, spooned and leveled. But here's what most conversion pages won't tell you: cocoa powder is a weird ingredient. It's had most of its fat pressed out. What's left is a very fine, very light powder that weighs about 20% less than the same volume of all-purpose flour. If you blindly treat it like flour (120g per cup), you'll add 20% too much — and your brownies will taste like they were baked in a chalk factory.

The Conversion Formula (Cups → Grams)

The math is simple, but the number you plug in depends on which cocoa you're using:

  • Grams = Cups × Grams-per-Cup
  • Natural Unsweetened Cocoa: 1 cup × 100 g/cup = 100 grams
  • Dutch-Process Cocoa: 1 cup × 100 g/cup = 100 grams
  • Black Cocoa: 1 cup × 95 g/cup = 95 grams

Natural vs Dutch-Process: Density Is the Same, Chemistry Is Not

Natural cocoa is acidic (pH ~5–6); Dutch-process cocoa has been treated with alkali to neutralize that acid (pH ~7–8). The alkalization makes it darker and mellower, but it doesn't change the density in any way that matters for your kitchen scale. Both clock in at 100 grams per cup. The real difference shows up in your leavening — natural cocoa reacts with baking soda (which needs acid to activate); Dutch cocoa doesn't. Use natural cocoa in a recipe that calls for baking soda but no other acid, and Dutch in recipes that use baking powder. Wrong cocoa + wrong leavener = flat, dense cake. The weight doesn't change; the chemistry does.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 cup of cocoa powder?

A1: One US customary cup of natural unsweetened cocoa powder weighs approximately 100 grams when spooned and leveled. Dutch-process cocoa is the same — 100 grams per cup. Black cocoa is slightly lighter at about 95 grams per cup due to finer milling.

Q2: Why is cocoa powder lighter than flour per cup?

A2: Cocoa powder is defatted — most of the cocoa butter (the fat) has been hydraulically pressed out during processing, leaving behind very fine, low-density solids. All-purpose flour weighs 120g per cup; cocoa weighs only 100g. If you substitute cocoa for flour by volume 1:1, you're getting less structural material and a different liquid-absorption profile — your baked good will be fragile and possibly dry.

Q3: Does Dutch-process cocoa weigh the same as natural cocoa?

A3: For all practical baking purposes, yes — both weigh 100 grams per US cup. The alkalization process changes color and pH but not density. Black cocoa (a heavily Dutch-processed variant) is the exception: it's more finely milled and weighs closer to 95 grams per cup.

Q4: Should I sift cocoa powder before measuring?

A4: Yes — and this matters more for cocoa than almost any other dry ingredient. Cocoa powder clumps aggressively, especially if it's been sitting in the pantry for a while. Those clumps pack unevenly and can throw your measurement off by 10–15%. Sift or at least whisk vigorously before spooning into your measuring cup. This converter assumes sifted, spooned, and leveled cocoa.
