1 Tablespoon of Honey to Grams — Precise Small Measures

Convert tablespoons of honey to grams — for tea, small-batch baking, and recipes where a sticky tablespoon just is not accurate enough.

Gram Equivalent:

21.25 grams (21.25 g)

*1 US tbsp honey ≈ 21.25g (340g per cup ÷ 16 tbsp per cup). Honey is about 42% heavier per tablespoon than water (14.8g/tbsp) due to its high dissolved sugar content.

1 Tablespoon Honey = 21 Grams. Here Is Why That Number Is Deceptively Important.

Most people do not weigh tablespoons of honey. They dip a spoon into the jar, let the golden thread spin itself into a coil, and call it a tablespoon. But a tablespoon of honey is not the same thing as a tablespoon of anything else. One US tablespoon of honey weighs about 21.25 grams — roughly 42% heavier than a tablespoon of water (14.8g), 55% heavier than a tablespoon of vegetable oil (13.6g), and substantially heavier than a tablespoon of maple syrup (about 20g). The density comes from the fact that honey is roughly 80% sugar dissolved in about 17% water, packing enormous mass into every milliliter. That extra density matters in three situations in particular:

  1. You are adjusting sweetness in a beverage. A teaspoon of honey in tea weighs about 7 grams and contributes roughly 21 calories. If you are tracking sugar intake and eyeballing the drizzle from a honey bear, you are almost certainly consuming more than you think — a "squeeze" can easily be 10-12 grams, or about a tablespoon and a half.
  2. You are scaling a small-batch recipe that calls for tablespoons. A recipe calling for 2 tablespoons of honey expects about 42 grams. If your tablespoon is a heaping home-style scoop rather than a leveled measure, you could be adding 50 grams or more — a 20% overpour that adds extra moisture, extra sweetness, and extra browning potential to a batter that was balanced for 42 grams.
  3. You are substituting honey for granulated sugar. Honey is about 1.4 times sweeter than table sugar by weight, but it also adds about 17% water to your dough or batter. If a recipe calls for 50 grams of sugar and you substitute 50 grams of honey, you have added roughly 8 grams of extra water and a disproportionate amount of sweetness. The substitution is more art than arithmetic, but weighing both ingredients is the only way to do the art part consistently. Does Honey Variety Change the Weight? The Short Answer Is No.

Clover honey, wildflower honey, orange blossom, tupelo, manuka — they all hover around 340 grams per cup and 21 grams per tablespoon. The floral source changes the flavor profile, the color, the acidity, and the trace enzyme content, but the sugar-to-water ratio — the thing that determines density — is remarkably consistent across varieties. Bees reduce nectar to roughly 17-18% moisture content regardless of which flowers they visited. The one practical exception is creamed or whipped honey, where controlled crystallization and incorporated air reduce the density slightly — about 320 grams per cup, or 20 grams per tablespoon. That 1-gram difference per tablespoon is at the edge of what most kitchen scales can measure reliably, so for practical purposes, treat all liquid honey as 21 grams per tablespoon. The bigger variable is not the honey type but the measuring technique: a leveled tablespoon versus a heaping tablespoon can differ by 5 grams or more, which dwarfs any varietal density difference. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Why does honey weigh more per tablespoon than water?

A1: Honey is roughly 80% sugar (fructose and glucose) dissolved in about 17% water. All that dissolved sugar dramatically increases the mass per milliliter. One tablespoon of water weighs about 14.8 grams; one tablespoon of honey weighs about 21.25 grams — roughly 42% heavier. The practical takeaway: never assume a tablespoon of honey behaves like a tablespoon of any water-based liquid in a recipe.

Q2: Does the type of honey — clover, wildflower, raw — affect the weight per tablespoon?

A2: No, not meaningfully. All liquid honey varieties hover around 340 grams per cup and 21 grams per tablespoon regardless of floral source, because the sugar-to-water ratio that determines density is consistent across varieties. Creamed or whipped honey is slightly lighter (about 20g/tbsp) due to incorporated air, but the difference is marginal. Measuring technique — leveled versus heaping spoon — matters far more than honey variety.

Q3: How do I accurately measure a tablespoon of honey without making a sticky mess?

A3: Lightly coat your measuring spoon with neutral oil or nonstick spray before scooping honey — it will slide out cleanly. But the better approach for baking is to skip the spoon entirely: place your mixing bowl on a kitchen scale, tare to zero, and pour honey directly into the bowl until the scale reads 21 grams per tablespoon called for. This avoids the mess, eliminates the heaping-versus-leveled variable, and saves you from washing a honey-coated spoon.
