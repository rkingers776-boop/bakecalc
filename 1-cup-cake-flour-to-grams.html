1 Cup Cake Flour to Grams Converter

Convert 1 US customary cup of cake flour into grams — the lighter, finer flour used for chiffon, sponge, and tender layer cakes.

Gram Equivalent:

110 grams (110 g)

*Calculated using standard US customary cup densities via spoon-and-level method.

How Many Grams in 1 Cup of Cake Flour?

If your recipe calls for one cup of cake flour and your scale is set to grams, you want 110 grams — the figure this converter uses. It assumes the spoon-and-level method: fluff the flour where it sits, spoon it into the cup until it heaps over the rim, then sweep a straight edge across the top.

Published charts put cake flour somewhere between 110 and 115 grams per cup, and that spread is not a mistake in anyone's math. Cake flour is milled finer than all-purpose and it holds more air, so how you fill the cup moves the number more than the flour itself does. Measure somewhere in that band and you are fine; the recipes that care about cake flour are not the ones where five grams decides the outcome.

Why Cake Flour Weighs Less Than All-Purpose Flour

Same cup, same method, about ten grams lighter than all-purpose. Two things cause it. Cake flour is milled to a finer particle size, so the particles do not pack as efficiently and more of the cup is air. It is also made from soft wheat at only 7–10% protein rather than the 10–12% in all-purpose, which makes for a softer, less structural flour.

Flip the method and the gap opens up fast. Scoop cake flour straight from the bag without fluffing it first and you can compress up to about 130 grams into that same cup — an 18% overshoot on a 110-gram target. In a chiffon or an angel food cake, where the whole structure depends on a delicate foam holding up a small amount of flour, that is the difference between a tall tender slice and a flat rubbery one. Ten seconds with a spoon prevents it. If you want to see exactly where the two flours part ways, we compared them side by side in cake flour vs all-purpose vs bread flour.

Does Sifting Change the Number?

Yes, and by more than most people expect. A sifted cup of cake flour reads closer to 100 grams than 110. That is why two bakers following the same recipe can end up ten grams apart per cup and neither one is wrong — one sifted before measuring, the other did not.

  • Recipe gives grams and says "sifted": sift, then weigh. The sifting is about aeration for the batter, not about the number.
  • Recipe gives cups and says "sifted": sift first, then measure the sifted flour. Measuring first and sifting afterwards gives you a lighter cup than the recipe author used.
  • Recipe says nothing: spoon and level without sifting, and use 110 g per cup.

Grams of Cake Flour Back to Cups

Divide the grams by 110 and you have your cups. The common amounts:

  • 1/4 cup ≈ 28 g · 1/3 cup ≈ 37 g · 1/2 cup = 55 g
  • 100 g ≈ just under 1 cup
  • 150 g ≈ 1⅓ cups
  • 200 g ≈ a shade over 1¾ cups
  • 250 g ≈ 2¼ cups

Those quarter, third, and half-cup figures are the ones worth keeping in your head, because they are what most cake recipes actually ask for. For the cup-based conversions across every other ingredient, the cooking converter covers the whole kitchen in one place.

Substituting All-Purpose Flour for Cake Flour

By weight, the safest swap is 110 g of all-purpose in place of 110 g of cake flour. You keep the hydration the recipe was built around and give up only some tenderness — that substitution fails far more often when people swap cup for cup than when they swap gram for gram.

The volume trick you will see repeated everywhere is 1 cup minus 2 tablespoons of all-purpose flour plus 2 tablespoons of cornstarch, whisked together. That pulls the protein down into cake flour's territory, and it works, but it is a workaround for people without cake flour rather than a true substitute. If the recipe is a chiffon or an angel food cake, buy the cake flour.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 cup of cake flour?

A1: About 110 grams using the spoon-and-level method, which is the figure this converter uses. Published charts range from 110 to 115 grams per cup, and a sifted cup can read closer to 100 grams.

Q2: Is cake flour lighter than all-purpose flour?

A2: Yes — roughly 110 g per cup against 120 g for all-purpose, measured the same way. A finer mill and a lower protein content (7–10% versus 10–12%) both leave more air in the cup.

Q3: How much does sifting change a cup of cake flour?

A3: Sifting can take a cup from about 110 grams down to roughly 100 grams. If your recipe asks for sifted cake flour, sift first and then measure or weigh.

Q4: Can I use all-purpose flour instead of cake flour?

A4: Yes, with care. Replacing 110 g of cake flour with 110 g of all-purpose keeps the recipe's hydration intact and costs you a little tenderness. For a closer match by volume, use 1 cup minus 2 tablespoons of all-purpose plus 2 tablespoons of cornstarch.
