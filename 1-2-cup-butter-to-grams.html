1/2 cup of butter in grams — the short answer

Half a cup of butter is 113 grams, which is exactly one US stick, which is 4 ounces, which is 8 tablespoons. Every one of those figures is the same quantity described a different way, and this is the one butter conversion that requires no arithmetic at all.

The reason is packaging. American butter is sold in quarter-pound sticks, and a quarter pound is 4 ounces, and 4 ounces of butter measures half a cup. So the wrapper doubles as the measuring tool, and the measurement problem disappears.

The One Figure Every Other Butter Conversion Comes From

If you remember one thing about butter measurement, it should be this: one stick equals half a cup, 113 grams, 8 tablespoons. Everything else on this site's butter pages is a multiple or a fraction of it.

  • 1/4 cup — half a stick, 57 g, 4 tbsp
  • 1/2 cup — one stick, 113 g, 8 tbsp
  • 1 cup — two sticks, 227 g, 16 tbsp
  • 1 pound — four sticks, 454 g, 2 cups

The 113 g figure is technically 113.4 g, which is an eighth of 907.2 g — the weight of a pound of butter expressed in grams. Rounding to 113 or 114 makes no practical difference, but the number is not arbitrary. It is what a quarter pound comes to.

Why Cutting Beats Measuring

Butter is the only common baking ingredient whose packaging is designed to remove the need for a measuring tool, and that design works better than any cup. Pressing solid butter into a measuring cup leaves air gaps, and how much depends entirely on how firmly it was pressed — two people can fill the same half cup with 100 g and 120 g respectively.

The wrapper sidesteps the problem by printing the tablespoons directly on the paper. To get half a cup, cut nothing: it is the whole stick. To get a quarter cup, cut at the 4 tablespoon mark. To get a tablespoon and a half, cut at the mark. The measurement is made by the paper, not by a jug.

Half a Cup of Butter in the Recipe

  • Cookie dough — half a stick to a full stick is typical for a small batch, creamed with an equal weight of sugar.
  • Single-layer cake — around 113 g of butter is the standard for one 8 or 9 inch round.
  • Buttercream — a small batch, enough to fill a single layer cake.
  • Pan sauce — half a stick is generous for finishing a sauce for four, and it is normally added cold at the end rather than cooked in.

Solid or Melted, the Weight Does Not Change

Half a cup of solid butter is 113 g. Melt it and it is still 113 g — melting does not remove anything. What changes is the volume: melted butter has no air gaps, so 113 g of melted butter occupies slightly less than half a cup, and a cup of melted butter weighs about 240 g rather than 227.

This matters because recipes specify which state they mean. If a recipe says half a cup of butter, melted, measure solid and melt it. If it says half a cup of melted butter, melt first and then measure the liquid. The two readings differ by around 5%, and in a recipe where the butter is the only fat, that is the difference between a crumb that holds together and one that does not.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1/2 cup of butter?

A1: 113 grams, which is exactly one US stick. Because American butter is sold in quarter-pound sticks, the pack itself is the measure — no scooping and no scale required.

Q2: How many tablespoons is 1/2 cup of butter?

A2: Eight. That is the figure printed on every US butter wrapper, with a mark for each tablespoon so the stick can be cut to any fraction without a measuring tool.

Q3: Is 1/2 cup of butter 4 ounces?

A3: Yes, 113 grams is 4 ounces by weight. It is worth separating that from fluid ounces: this is an ounce of mass, not of volume. Four fluid ounces of a liquid would be 118 millilitres, a different quantity entirely.
