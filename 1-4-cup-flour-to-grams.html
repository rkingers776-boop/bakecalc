How Many Grams in 1/4 Cup of Flour?

The quarter-cup is the smallest standard dry-measure increment in most American measuring cup sets — and ¼ cup of all-purpose flour equals 30 grams. That's one quarter of the 120 g full-cup standard. Bread flour comes in slightly higher at ~33 g per quarter cup, while cake flour is lighter at ~28 g.

The Conversion Formula

One quarter of the full-cup density for each flour type:

  • Grams = Cups × Grams-per-Cup
  • All-Purpose Flour: 0.25 × 120 g = 30 grams
  • Bread Flour: 0.25 × 130 g = 33 grams (rounded)
  • Cake Flour: 0.25 × 110 g = 28 grams (rounded)

Where You'll See 1/4 Cup Flour in Recipes

The quarter-cup flour measure shows up in several specific baking contexts: thickening a roux or gravy, dusting a work surface for rolling dough, small-batch recipes like mug cakes or two-person desserts, and as a minor binder in fillings and custards. Because the quantities are small, a 3–5 gram error from scooping vs. spooning represents a proportionally larger mistake — all the more reason to use a scale for precision at this scale.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1/4 cup of all-purpose flour?

A1: One quarter cup (¼ US cup) of all-purpose flour weighs approximately 30 grams — exactly one quarter of the standard 120 g full-cup baseline used in professional baking.

Q2: How can I measure 30 grams of flour without a scale?

A2: Use a ¼ cup dry measure with the spoon-and-level method: lightly spoon flour into the cup until mounded, then sweep level with a straight edge. This yields roughly 30 g of all-purpose flour.

Q3: When do recipes use a 1/4 cup of flour?

A3: Quarter-cup measures appear in small-batch baking, dusting surfaces, thickening roux and sauces, and as a minor structural ingredient in custards and fillings where a full half-cup would be too much.
