200g Powdered Sugar to Cups Converter

Convert 200 grams of powdered sugar into cups — one and two thirds, or a clean two cups if it goes through a sieve first.

Cup Equivalent:

Roughly 1 2/3 cups (1.67 cups)

*Calculated using standard US customary culinary measuring cups for unsifted powdered sugar.

200 g of powdered sugar in cups — the short answer

Two hundred grams of powdered sugar is 1 2/3 cups, or 1.67 cups, which you can measure as 1 1/2 cups plus 2 tablespoons plus 2 teaspoons — 26 level tablespoons and a couple of teaspoons, which lands on 200 g exactly. Unsifted, at 120 grams per cup, that is the answer.

1 2/3 Cups, or Exactly 2 if You Sift

As with every powdered sugar conversion, the sieve changes the volume without touching the weight:

  • 200 g, unsifted — 1.67 cups
  • 200 g, sifted — 2.00 cups

A third of a cup of apparent difference from a step that removed nothing. If a recipe says two cups of powdered sugar and you weigh out 200 g without sifting, the recipe wanted the sifted version and you have given it a fifth more sugar than it meant. The frosting will be stiffer and sweeter, and adding liquid to fix the texture will thin the flavour along with it.

The 27 g Between a Scoop and a Spoon

At 200 g the compressibility of powdered sugar stops being a curiosity and starts being the dominant variable. Three ordinary ways to fill a cup:

  • Spooned in and levelled — about 110 g per cup, so 200 g takes 1.8 cups
  • Scooped from the bag and levelled — about 120 g per cup, so 200 g takes 1.67 cups
  • Scooped, tapped, and levelled — 135 g per cup or more, so 200 g takes 1.5 cups

That is a spread of nearly a third of a cup between the loosest and the firmest method, or about 27 g at this quantity. In a buttercream, 27 g is the difference between a frosting that pipes a soft rosette and one that holds a sharp edge. Weigh it if the shape matters.

The Cornstarch Content at 200 g

Powdered sugar runs 3 to 5% cornstarch, which at 200 g means 6 to 10 g — somewhere between two and three teaspoons. That starch is why a powdered sugar icing sets to a dry, sliceable surface and why the same icing made with blended granulated sugar stays tacky.

It also puts a ceiling on substitution. Cornstarch absorbs water and thickens when heated, so the more powdered sugar a recipe uses, the more noticeable the starch becomes. Above about 200 g in a liquid-based icing, the starch starts to read as a slightly chalky finish, and that is when a recipe moves to a cooked or meringue-based frosting instead.

200 g Is Close to Half a Box

A one-pound box of powdered sugar is 454 g and roughly 3 3/4 cups unsifted. So 200 g is a little under half the box, and the cups work out in round numbers:

  • 1 cup — 120 g
  • 2 cups — 240 g, sifted would be 200 g
  • 3 cups — 360 g
  • 3 3/4 cups — 450 g, the whole box

What 200 g of Powdered Sugar Frosts

  • One 8 inch layer cake — with 100 g of soft butter and 2 tablespoons of cream, enough for the top and sides.
  • One 9 by 13 sheet cake — the same batch, spread only on top, with a little left over.
  • A dozen doughnuts or a tray of buns — 200 g with 4 tablespoons of milk, dipped and drained.
  • A single batch of no-bake cheesecake filling — 200 g with 200 g of cream cheese, where the sugar is structural as well as sweet.

Powdered Sugar Against Granulated at the Same Weight

Same 200 g, very different jobs. Granulated sugar at 200 g is one cup, and in a cake batter it dissolves into the water phase and contributes bulk, tenderness, and browning. Powdered sugar at 200 g is 1 2/3 cups, dissolves on contact, and brings its cornstarch along.

That is why the two are rarely interchangeable at the same weight. Powdered sugar has no grit to cream against butter, so it cannot aerate a batter the way granulated does — the crystals are not there to cut into the fat. It belongs where the sugar is not asked to do structural work, which is broadly the finishing end of baking: icings, glazes, dustings, and fillings.

A1: 1 2/3 cups, or 1.67 cups, which is 26 level tablespoons plus 2 teaspoons. The calculation uses 120 grams of unsifted powdered sugar per cup.

Q2: Is 200g of powdered sugar the same as 2 cups?

A2: Only if the sugar is sifted. Sifted powdered sugar sits at about 100 grams per cup, so 200 grams fills two cups exactly. Unsifted, the same weight fills about 1 2/3 cups.

Q3: How much frosting does 200g of powdered sugar make?

A3: With 100 grams of soft butter it frosts the top and sides of one 8 inch layer cake, or the top of a 9 by 13 inch sheet cake. Add liquid a teaspoon at a time to loosen it.
