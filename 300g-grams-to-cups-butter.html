Scaling 300 Grams of Butter for Bulk Recipe Batches

Working with 300 grams of butter means you are likely scaling a rich pastry dough, brioche, or large cake batch — roughly 1 cup plus about 2.5 tablespoons (1.32 cups). Since standard US butter sticks cap at 113.4g each, and a cup of solid butter maxes out at 227g, larger butter weights require multi-stick and multi-cup calculations for accuracy.

The Mathematical Conversion Formula

The calculation divides butter mass by the density constant based on physical state:

  • Total Cup Volume = Weight in Grams ÷ Butter Component Per Cup
  • Stick Butter: 300g ÷ 227g/cup = 1.32 cups
  • Melted Butter: 300g ÷ 240g/cup = 1.25 cups
  • Whipped Butter: 300g ÷ 150g/cup = 2.00 cups

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 300g of standard solid butter?

A1: 300 grams of standard stick butter equals approximately 1.32 cups — about 1 cup plus 2.5 tablespoons.

Q2: How many sticks of butter do I need for 300g?

A2: Use 2 full US sticks (226.8g) plus roughly 65% of a third stick for 300g. This equals about 1 cup plus 2.5 tablespoons of solid butter.

Q3: Does melted butter measure differently than solid butter at 300g?

A3: If melted, 300g of butter occupies roughly 1.25 cups (240g/cup). Stick to solid measurements for accuracy unless the recipe specifies melted.
