1 Cup of Cornstarch to Grams — The Squeaky Powder That's Heavier Than Flour

Cornstarch packs tighter than flour — 128g per cup. How to convert it, what it does in baking, and the UK "cornflour" confusion sorted.

Gram Equivalent:

128 grams (128 g)

*1 US cup cornstarch = 128g. 1 tbsp ≈ 8g. Finer than flour → packs tighter → heavier per cup.

1 Cup Cornstarch = 128 Grams. That's 8 Grams More Than a Cup of Flour.

Cornstarch is weird stuff. Rub it between your fingers and it squeaks. Mix it with a little water and it forms a solid that liquifies when you stop pressing — the stuff of elementary school science experiments. And at 128 grams per cup, it weighs more than all-purpose flour (120g) even though it looks and feels lighter. The paradox is real: cornstarch is less dense as individual particles (1.5 g/cm³ for pure starch vs 1.5–1.6 for flour proteins), but those particles are so fine and uniform that they pack together with far less air between them. The result is a higher bulk density in your measuring cup. More starch per scoop, essentially.

This packing difference has consequences. If a gluten-free recipe calls for "1 cup cornstarch" and you substitute "1 cup flour" thinking they're close, you're off by 8 grams — about 7% less dry material. Gluten-free baking is already finicky enough without adding a 7% measurement error.

Three Jobs Cornstarch Does in Baking (and Why the Weight Matters for Each)

  • Thickening. A tablespoon of cornstarch (8g) will thicken about 1 cup of liquid to a gravy consistency. Double that for a pie filling that needs to hold its shape when sliced. The weight matters because cornstarch thickening power is proportional to mass, not volume — 8 grams of cornstarch has the same thickening power regardless of whether you bought it last week or six months ago, whereas a "heaping tablespoon" could be 6g or 12g depending on who's scooping.
  • Tenderizing. Replacing 2 tablespoons of flour (15g) with 2 tablespoons of cornstarch (16g) in a cake or cookie recipe inhibits gluten formation and produces a more tender crumb. This is the secret behind "melt-in-your-mouth" shortbread and ultra-tender cakes. The 1-gram difference between the flour and cornstarch in that swap is negligible — the magic is in the chemistry, not the weight.
  • Coating for frying. Cornstarch creates a crisper, lighter crust than flour because it's pure starch with no protein to brown and toughen. For fried chicken or tofu, a 50/50 mix of cornstarch and flour (by weight — 64g of each per cup of dry coating mix) gives you the best of both: crispness from the starch, color and flavor from the flour.

US "Cornstarch" vs UK "Cornflour" — Quick Detour

In the United States, "cornstarch" is the fine white powder at 128g per cup. In the UK, Australia, and New Zealand, the same product is "cornflour." But in the US, "corn flour" (two words, sometimes "masa harina" or finely ground cornmeal) is a different product — whole ground corn, yellowish, and much heavier at about 140–150g per cup. If you're working from a British recipe that says "cornflour," use cornstarch at 128g/cup. If an American recipe says "corn flour," it probably means finely ground cornmeal, not cornstarch. Check the color: white powder = cornstarch; yellow powder = corn flour/masa. Getting this wrong will ruin a recipe faster than any other mistake on this page. I've done it. The cake was not good.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1 cup of cornstarch?

1 US cup of cornstarch = 128 grams. One tablespoon weighs approximately 8 grams (128 ÷ 16). One teaspoon weighs approximately 2.7 grams.

Q2: Why is cornstarch heavier per cup than flour?

Cornstarch particles are finer and more uniform than wheat flour particles, so they pack together with less trapped air. Flour at 120g/cup has roughly 17% more air space between particles. The individual starch molecules are actually lighter than flour proteins — it's the packing, not the density of the material itself, that makes the cup measurement heavier.

Q3: Can I substitute cornstarch for flour 1:1?

No — not even close. Cornstarch is pure starch with no gluten, no protein structure. If you replace all the flour in a recipe with cornstarch by weight, you'll get something closer to pudding than bread. In gluten-free baking, cornstarch is typically 20–30% of the total flour blend, mixed with other flours that provide structure. And even then, use the 128g/cup weight — not the 120g/cup of wheat flour.
