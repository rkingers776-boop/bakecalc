The Culinary Density Profile of 150 Grams of Sugar

Baking is an exact science where mass and volume must align perfectly. Converting 150 grams of sugar to cups depends heavily on structural density. For instance, coarse granulated sugar packs down tighter than fine, aerated powdered sugar. This means 150g of white sugar occupies a much smaller physical volume in a measuring cup than the same weight of confectioners sugar.

The Mathematical Conversion Formula

The volumetric extraction requires dividing the absolute mass by the known ingredient substance constants:

  • Total Cup Volume = Given Mass in Grams ÷ Base Weight Component Per Cup
  • Granulated: 150g ÷ 200g/cup = 0.75 cups (3/4 cup)
  • Powdered: 150g ÷ 120g/cup = 1.25 cups (1 1/4 cups)
  • Packed Brown: 150g ÷ 220g/cup = 0.68 cups (roughly 2/3 cup)

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 150g of granulated white sugar?

A1: 150 grams of traditional granulated white sugar equals exactly 0.75 cups, which fits precisely into a standard 3/4 measuring cup.

Q2: How do I measure 150g of powdered sugar in cups?

A2: Powdered sugar is much lighter. 150 grams translates to 1.25 cups, meaning you will need 1 full cup plus an additional 1/4 cup.

Q3: Should I sift my sugar before or after measuring 150 grams?

A3: If a recipe specifies grams, weigh it directly on a scale. If you must use cup volumes, always sift powdered sugar before spooning it into the measuring cup to avoid dense packing errors.
