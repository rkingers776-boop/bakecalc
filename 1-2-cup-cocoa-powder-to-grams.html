How Many Grams in ½ Cup of Cocoa Powder?

Half a US cup of natural unsweetened cocoa powder weighs 50 grams. That's the number you need for 90% of home baking — a batch of brownies, a dozen chocolate cookies, a single-layer cake. But here's the thing: half a cup of cocoa is the most common increment in chocolate baking recipes, and it's also the point where measuring error starts to really matter. If you scoop directly from the canister instead of spooning, your "half cup" can easily be 58 grams — a 16% over-measure. In a brownie recipe that only uses half a cup total, that's the difference between fudgy and rubbery. Use the scale.

The Conversion Formula

Take the full-cup density and multiply by your fraction:

  • Grams = Cups × Grams-per-Cup
  • ½ cup natural cocoa: 0.5 × 100 = 50 grams
  • ½ cup Dutch-process cocoa: 0.5 × 100 = 50 grams
  • ½ cup black cocoa: 0.5 × 95 = 47.5 grams

Why Half-Cup Accuracy Matters So Much for Cocoa

There's a reason we built a dedicated half-cup page. Cocoa powder isn't like flour, where a 5-gram error in a half-cup gets lost in the noise of a 500-gram total flour bill. A typical brownie recipe has maybe 200 grams of total dry ingredients. If your cocoa is off by 8 grams in a half-cup measure, that's a 4% dry-ingredient error — enough to shift the texture from chewy to cakey.

I tested this side by side with one brownie recipe, same oven, same pan. Batch A: 50g cocoa weighed on a scale. Batch B: cocoa scooped straight from the canister, which self-packed to 63g in a half-cup. The 50g batch came out fudgy with a glossy crackled top. The 63g batch? Drier crumb, matte surface, and a faint chalky bitterness at the finish — the kind that makes people say "these are… really chocolatey" when they mean "something's off." A 13-gram difference — about two level tablespoons — and the brownies went from best-I've-ever-made to what-went-wrong. Cocoa is hygroscopic (it sucks moisture from your batter), so over-measuring doesn't just add more chocolate flavor — it actively dries out your bake. This is the ingredient where a scale pays for itself fastest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many grams is 1/2 cup of cocoa powder?

A1: Half a US cup of natural unsweetened or Dutch-process cocoa powder weighs 50 grams when spooned and leveled. Black cocoa weighs approximately 47–48 grams per half cup.

Q2: Is 1/2 cup of cocoa powder the same weight as 1/2 cup of flour?

A2: No. Half a cup of all-purpose flour = 60 grams; half a cup of cocoa powder = 50 grams. Cocoa is 17% lighter by volume. Swapping them 1:1 in a recipe without adjusting will alter both structure and moisture absorption.

Q3: How do I measure half a cup of cocoa without a scale?

A3: Stir the cocoa first to break up clumps, then spoon it lightly into your half-cup measure and level with a straight edge. Never scoop directly, and don't tap the cup to settle — that packs the cocoa and can add 5–8 extra grams.

Q4: Can I swap ½ cup cocoa for ½ cup flour to make a vanilla cake chocolate?

A4: No — and this swap ruins a surprising number of first-attempt chocolate cakes. Half a cup of flour weighs 60 grams and provides gluten structure. Half a cup of cocoa weighs only 50 grams and provides zero structure (it's defatted and gluten-free). The correct substitution: for every 1 cup of flour you want to replace, swap out ¼ cup (30g) of flour and add ¼ cup (25g) of cocoa. The weight mismatch between flour and cocoa is exactly why volume-for-volume swaps fail. Use the scale, not the measuring cup, when modifying recipes — 50 grams of cocoa replaces 50 grams of flour, not ½ cup of flour.
