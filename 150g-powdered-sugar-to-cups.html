*Calculated using standard US customary culinary measuring cups for unsifted powdered sugar.

150 g of powdered sugar in cups — the short answer

One hundred and fifty grams of powdered sugar is 1 1/4 cups, or 1.25 cups, or 20 level tablespoons. Unsifted, at 120 grams per cup, that is the whole of the arithmetic. If your recipe says confectioners sugar or icing sugar, it means this same ingredient, and the same numbers apply.

What makes 150 g a more interesting amount than it looks is the packaging.

Why 1 1/4 Cups Is an Awkward Answer

In a round cake recipe the sugar tends to come in one of two forms: one cup, or three cups. One and a quarter sits between them, and it forces a two-measure job — a full cup, then a quarter cup — where the recipe author probably intended you to reach for something else entirely. Three quarters of the time, a recipe calling for 1 1/4 cups of powdered sugar is a recipe written by volume that a developer converted from a 150 g original, and the fraction is an artefact of the conversion rather than a considered choice.

So measure the 150 g if you have a scale, and if you do not, take 20 level tablespoons rather than trying to eyeball a quarter cup on top of a full one.

A One-Pound Box Is 3 3/4 Cups

The standard supermarket box of powdered sugar is one pound, or 454 grams. Unsifted, that box holds about 3.78 cups, which is close enough to 3 3/4 cups that the box and the volume agree.

That gives you a useful set of mental fractions:

  • 1/4 box — 113 g, about 1 cup
  • 1/3 box — 151 g, about 1 1/4 cups
  • 1/2 box — 227 g, about 1 7/8 cups
  • The whole box — 454 g, about 3 3/4 cups

So 150 g is almost exactly a third of a box. If you are making icing for one cake and you know you will use a third of the bag, you also know you have two thirds left — which is the more useful piece of information.

The Sifting Gap at 150 g

Sift it and the same 150 g swells from 1 1/4 cups to 1 1/2 cups. Half a cup of extra volume, produced by nothing at all, and it is the reason two people can follow the same frosting recipe and get two different consistencies.

The practical rule is to notice which density the recipe assumes. Old American cookbooks generally assume unsifted, because the box was the tool. Modern recipes written in grams almost always assume sifted, because a scale made volume irrelevant and the author stopped thinking about it. When a modern recipe says 1 1/2 cups of powdered sugar alongside metric weights elsewhere in the list, that 1 1/2 cups is a sifted measure, and reading it as unsifted will add 25 g of sugar to the bowl.

Royal Icing and the 150 g Batch

Royal icing is where this amount earns its keep. The classic starting ratio is one egg white to about 150 g of powdered sugar, beaten until the icing holds a soft peak and then thinned a teaspoon of water at a time to the consistency you need.

  • Outline consistency — about 150 g to 1 egg white, left thick.
  • Flood consistency — the same base, thinned until a ribbon disappears in 10 seconds.
  • Petal or lace work — thinned further, and the cornstarch in the sugar is what lets it hold a fine edge as it dries.

One egg white is about 30 g, so 150 g of sugar makes a batch weighing a little under 180 g — more than enough for a few dozen cookies and not enough to waste.

Storing It So It Stays Measurable

Powdered sugar takes on moisture and develops hard lumps in a humid kitchen. Once lumpy, it stops measuring predictably: a cup might come in at 130 g because the lumps leave gaps, or at 145 g because you packed it down trying to level the top.

Keep it in an airtight container rather than the folded paper box, and if it has already hardened, a few seconds in a food processor restores it without changing the weight. Sieving works too, but it changes the volume, so sift before you measure, not after.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How many cups is 150g of powdered sugar?

A1: 1 1/4 cups, or 1.25 cups, which is 20 level tablespoons. The figure is for unsifted powdered sugar at 120 grams per cup.

Q2: Is 150g of powdered sugar a third of a box?

A2: Yes. A standard one-pound box is 454 grams, which is about 3 3/4 cups unsifted, so a 150 gram portion is very close to one third of the box.

Q3: How much does 150g of powdered sugar weigh after sifting?

A3: It still weighs 150 grams, but it takes up half a cup more room — 1 1/2 cups rather than 1 1/4 — because sifting lowers the density to about 100 grams per cup.
